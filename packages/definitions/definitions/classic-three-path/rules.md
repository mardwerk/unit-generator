# Classic three-path rules

This is a BTD6-inspired content contract. It does not claim compatibility with BTD6 or prove that a consuming engine implements these rules. The output schema is the existing UnitSpec 0.1 structure, interpreted under this versioned definition.

A unit occupies one placement and has a positive purchase cost in credits. Range and geometry use world units, damage uses hit points, and durations and cooldowns use seconds. The configured numeric bands are authoritative. Unknown mechanics require a separate definition change.

Actions separate trigger, targeting, delivery, emitters, timing and effects. Interval actions repeat no faster than the greater of trigger.intervalSeconds and timing.cooldownSeconds, after their windup. For speed upgrades, set the interval scheduling floor low and let cooldown express the intended cadence. Per-emitter timing counts every emitter; aggregate timing shares a cycle. Every projectile/target application has finite caps.

Targeting selects enemies directly. Empty includeTags means unrestricted enemy tags; there is no implicit `enemy` tag. Excluded tags always disqualify a target. `self` selects exactly one target, needs no enemy and can use zero range. Priorities choose the eligible target ordering; a consuming game must define deterministic tie-breaking. A projectile applies at most maximumTargetsPerProjectile hits. Direct strikes, beams, areas and zones still obey explicit timing and target caps. Projectile, homing-projectile and arc-projectile deliveries require projectileSpeedWorldUnitsPerSecond. Aura, zone and trap deliveries require radiusWorldUnits. Every zone and trap also requires lifetimeSeconds, including actions introduced by an upgrade. Summon delivery requires a spawn effect.

A chain starts at the acquired target and continues to distinct eligible targets within the action's range. It cannot revisit a target or hit the original target again during that emission. The smaller of targeting.maximumTargets and delivery.maximumTargetsPerProjectile caps total targets, including the first. Every hit applies the same effect amounts; this definition has no damage falloff parameter or separate jump-distance parameter. A new mechanic with either behavior requires changing the contract. Line and arc delivery describe a delivery shape; obstacle and collision handling remain unchecked engine behavior.

Effects may deal direct damage, damage over time, apply declared statuses, change resources, spawn declared summons, move targets, reveal targets, trigger secondary actions, or alter credit economy. Healing, shields, generic stat modifiers and runtime transformations are excluded from this default. Status duration, maximum stacks, refresh/stack behavior and expiry are explicit. Status immunity/resistance belongs in supplied game context; checks do not invent boss immunity. Manual status removal is unsupported.

Resources declare starting amounts, capacity, ownership/persistence, generation, spending, insufficiency behavior and recovery. Time and on-hit generation have explicit rates or linked action IDs. On-damage generation is unsupported. Capacity is finite; consumers cannot refer to missing or unavailable resources. Spending never grants an unbounded chain of secondary effects. Secondary-action and summon references must resolve, and recursive action execution cycles are forbidden in this definition. This restriction is not a global rule for other definitions.

Active abilities are manually invoked through their declared action, costs, charges and cooldown. Automatic abilities use interval execution. Passive/reactive scheduling is unsupported. Manual actions must belong to an available ability. The configured manual-ability limit counts native active abilities.

There are exactly three independent upgrade paths with five cumulative tiers each. A legal selection uses one primary path through tier 5 and at most one other path through tier 2, with at most seven selected nodes. Prerequisites are inside the owning path. Prices are positive and strictly increase through a path. A tier applies changes to the current build, not a complete replacement copy of the tower.

Each mutable action/effect/resource/ability property belongs to one path. Independently selectable nodes cannot write the same property, even with multiplication, addition, or equal values. Prerequisite-linked tiers may change it again. A node writes a property once. Grant or enable each dependency before using it, and avoid replacing an action another path edits. Every legal selection, including available forms, must compile within numeric bounds.

Forms are encounter configurations, not runtime transformations. They are explicitly selected after an external unlock or an owning grant-form upgrade. Persistence is the encounter, activation is external, reversion is none, and requirements are empty. State transfer during live transformation is not defined because that mechanic is unsupported.

The ordinary validator checks structure, reference integrity, declared lifecycles, path independence, legal selections, numeric bands and supported request constraints. It does not simulate combat, prove prose matches mechanics, evaluate player enjoyment, or establish numerical balance. Acquisition cost, enemy composition, board space and roster interactions require game context and separate evaluation.

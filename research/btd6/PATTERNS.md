# BTD6 progression patterns

BTD6's useful pattern is a small recognizable foundation, an exclusive specialization, and a capstone that materially develops that specialization. Fifteen legal upgrade rows alone do not produce that structure. The following analysis covers every name and effect summary in the supplied [26-tower, 390-upgrade reference](raw/btd6_towers.json), together with the [six detailed profiles](BTD6-UNIT-EXAMPLES.md). It does not claim a current patch audit or complete output measurements. The snapshot includes newer and unusually complex towers; roster membership in this supplied export is not independent release verification.

## What the reference supports

T1 and T2 usually improve a foundation that remains useful as a crosspath. Dart's pierce, speed and range/detection lines make distinct offers. Sniper separates damage, detection/shrapnel and firing rate. These are much more legible than three differently named T1 purchases granting the same damage boost. However, early purchases can introduce an automatic subsystem: Wizard Fireball and Wall of Fire, Engineer Sentry Gun, Ninja Caltrops and Druid Heart of Thunder. Beast Handler purchases establish separate creature branches at T1. The correct rule is focused purpose and a small total early kit, not exclusively numeric upgrades or exactly one effect per row. Related range, projectile speed and pierce changes can serve one purpose. Source: the named tower records, codes `100`, `200`, `010`, `020`, `001`, `002`; [baseline complexity discussion](BTD6-UNIT-DESIGN.md).

T3 has a structural commitment role because only one path can exceed T2. The signature can be a new delivery system, such as Dart Spike-o-pult, Ice Cryo Cannon or Dartling Buckshot; a target specialization, such as Bomb MOAB Mauler; or a substantial improvement to the established loop, such as Sniper Deadly Precision, Engineer Sprockets or Buccaneer Destroyer. Requiring every T3 to invent a new operator would misread these examples. By T3, the player should understand what choosing this branch gains and what the other advanced paths would have supplied. Source: [crosspath snapshot](source-snapshots/btd6-crosspath.txt) and each tower's three tier-three records.

T4 often establishes the major path payoff and T5 makes that payoff exceptional. Dart's Juggernaut becomes a source of smaller Juggernaut balls. Bomb's Bloon Impact expands control eligibility at Bloon Crush. Glue Strike becomes repeated Glue Storm coverage. Alchemist's Stronger Stimulant becomes persistent Permanent Brew. Engineer's Overclock gains permanent stacking effects at Ultraboost. Comanche Defense's temporary support becomes permanent at Comanche Commander. These are different axes of improvement, not interchangeable damage multipliers. The final tier can add a related mechanism, but does not ordinarily grant all other paths' capstones. Source: the corresponding `400/500`, `040/050` and `004/005` records.

Three paths need different reasons to be purchased, not necessarily different role labels. Tack's fire ring, activated blades and dense tacks all damage groups but differ in geometry, output timing and player action. Banana Farm's production, bank and collection paths remain economy while differing in investment and management. Conversely, one path may combine roles: Elite Sniper provides attacks, income and allied support. `basic_dps` means reliable direct damage without a stronger specialization in the editorial taxonomy. It does not mean weak, beginner, unupgraded or low tier. Source: [role definitions](raw/towerdefense_categories.json) and the Tack, Farm and Sniper records.

Middle-path T4 manual abilities form a strong recurrent template across the roster, including Dart, Bomb, Tack, Glue, Sniper, Sub, Buccaneer, Ace, Heli, Mortar, Dartling, Wizard, Super, Ninja, Alchemist, Druid, Mermonkey, Farm, Spike, Village, Engineer and Beast Handler. It is not a rule that every path needs a button, or that every cooldown entry is a player command. Dark Knight unlocks Darkshift on bottom T3, Desperado Deadeye includes a T3 support command, Bomb Blitz is event-triggered, and Skywarden has unusual control interactions. Targeting a Mortar, moving an Ace flight pattern or selecting Sub submerge mode is configuration, not automatically another timed combat ability. Preserve automatic, triggered, activated, configuration and collection behavior separately. Source: [71 ability/control records](raw/btd6_abilities.csv), [ability snapshots](source-snapshots/btd6-activated-abilities.txt), and the individual upgrade summaries. Only six cooldowns are numerically verified in the package.

Naming often tracks the visible mechanism: Sharp Shots to Razor Sharp Shots; Fireball to Wall of Fire to Dragon's Breath to Phoenix; Faster Production to Spike Storm to Carpet of Spikes. Some paths signal rank, some weapon replacement, and some size or intensity. A generated character can follow the same principle with source-grounded names and a continuous weapon, posture, element or companion motif. Visual changes should reveal the purchased specialization and increasing commitment. This is an art-direction inference from names and described mechanisms, not a sprite audit: the raw package explicitly does not extract artwork or a complete visual progression. A costume name or image prompt cannot authorize mechanics that the build has not purchased.

## Prices do not establish output multipliers

Across all 78 paths, 77 have fixed Medium T4 and T5 incremental prices. Their T5/T4 price ratios range from 1.579 for Elite Sniper to 19.643 for Bloon Crush, with median 6.25. Monkeyopolis is excluded because its T5 price is conditional. These values can be reproduced by dividing `paths[].upgrades[4].incremental_cost.by_difficulty.medium` by the preceding tier's matching field in [raw JSON](raw/btd6_towers.json), excluding nulls. They are ratios of individual purchases, not cumulative build costs, affordability or measured power.

The package supplies no normalized post-upgrade combat-stat series. Prices exclude crosspaths, discounts, sacrifices, extra Beast Handlers and other investments. There is therefore no evidential basis here for a universal BTD6 rule such as T5 always deals four times T4 damage. A control expansion, permanent buff, wider coverage or income change also cannot be judged solely by personal DPS. The observed design supports a noticeable, branch-specific final payoff. Its numeric threshold in this Tool is an editable design convention requiring validation.

## Two separate restrictions

A single ordinary placed tower selects at most two paths, with only one exceeding T2. This is the per-build rule that permits `5-2-0` and rejects `3-3-0` or `1-1-1`. It does not mean that a player may own only one tier-five tower of any kind.

The ordinary T5 ownership rule limits a player to one instance of each particular tower type and T5 path across placed copies. One player can normally own the same tower type's three different T5s on three separate copies. This is a scene-level ownership rule, separate from legal tiers on one copy. Exceptions require explicit rules, such as the [Dart snapshot's Master Double Cross](source-snapshots/btd6-dart-2026-09-20.txt), which allows two Crossbow Masters. The retained crosspath and Dart extracts support the distinction and exception; they are not a complete current-mode rules audit. The generator has no placed-roster context, so it cannot presently prove T5 uniqueness across a player's scene. A future roster validator needs owner, tower-type, path and mode/knowledge exceptions.

## Practical generator checks

These recommendations distinguish source observations from the new default Profile's authoring policy. They are not claims that every semantic review or scene check is implemented. [Mechanics documentation](../../docs/MECHANICS.md) records the shipped contract. A Profile may promote a convention to a hard authoring constraint, but should state that choice explicitly.

| Concern | Deterministic evidence | Semantic review or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate early purchases | Compare normalized resolved T1 mechanic deltas across paths, excluding names and prose. Flag exact duplicates. | Shared parameter dimensions are not inherently duplicates if scope, tradeoffs or magnitudes differ. Cosmetic differences cannot rescue identical purchases. |
| Path differentiation | Compare pure T3, T4 and T5 builds for output, target capacity, cadence, reach, target access, control and active signatures. | Explain a different deployment reason for each path. Distinct role labels alone prove nothing. |
| Capstone payoff | Compare T4 and T5 on the declared branch axis and retain the measured delta. Check ordinary output separately from active peak and availability. | The current default compares the purchase with its preceding tier and affordable lower-tier alternatives, without a universal multiplier. Custom Profiles can still impose a ratio gate; that is not a measured BTD6 law or balance guarantee. Control, access, support and economy need their own criteria. Never demand invented damage to pass a support capstone. |
| Active burden | Count independent manual activations, their unlocks and cooldown groups in legal builds. | One dedicated manual-boost path is a useful starter convention. A second active path needs a different purpose and explicit exception, not another renamed damage boost. |
| Coherence | Resolve every legal build and check inheritance, missing prerequisites and replaced attacks. | Review whether the combined early crosspath kit preserves a weakness and develops the character's motif. |

For the current small combat vocabulary, direct damage divided by interval is only a local single-target proxy. Projectile count is meaningful only under the declared volley targeting semantics; multiplying pierce by DPS is an upper-bound group-capacity proxy, not realized damage. An active's duration/cooldown ratio estimates availability only when activation, overlap and scheduling assumptions hold. Do not turn these estimates into claims about simulated waves, enemy resistances, travel, map layout or game-wide balance.

The current default plans the character repertoire and three purchasing reasons before authoring mechanics. Broad specialization labels may repeat. It preserves T1/T2 attack identity, rejects duplicate T1 and capstone behavior, and permits at most one manual boost path. T3 normally specializes the attack, but need not add a new operator; T5 must justify its ultimate purchasing role without a universal 3x requirement. Custom Profiles can opt into those stronger structural gates. Existing saved Definitions retain their earlier contract. Different labels and passing ratios do not establish different gameplay.

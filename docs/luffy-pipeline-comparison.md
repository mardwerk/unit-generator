# Luffy pipeline comparison

This study compares eight generation prototypes with the existing classic workflow. Every live model stage used `gpt-5.6-luna`. Astra agents built separate implementations and independently reviewed the generated units. The final combined revision is available as an opt-in experiment, alongside the simpler compiler. It does not replace the default generator.

Implementation and commands are in [the experiment directory](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/README.md). [Recorded outcomes](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/evidence/comparison.md) retain failed attempts, candidate artifacts, calls, tokens and timings. [Machine-readable evidence](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/evidence/summary.json) separates validity, execution observations and accounting. None is an overall quality score.

The fresh final reviewer preferred the [refined high brief unit](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/examples/luffy-brief.unit.json) as the strongest new development candidate. It uses direct melee, Haki paths, three earned Gear forms and per-emitter scheduling that makes its added emitters produce additional hits. The [earlier medium alternative](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/examples/luffy-brief-alternative.unit.json) preserves broader fantasy coverage, including a separate prepared Gear Third attack and Observation reveal. Both have limitations. Neither implements the complete requested Gear system.

The [independent final recommendation](../experiments/luffy-pipelines/evidence/reviewer-final/final-recommendation.md) compares nine accepted candidates by separate dimensions. The earlier reviewer preferred the medium alternative; the fresh reviewer favored the revised high candidate's cleaner progression and caveats. These are different design preferences, not conflicting validity results.

## Results

The final revision produced four accepted units from four fresh attempts, with no planning correction or outer repair needed. Every run used three Luna calls. The original combined version accepted five of eight attempts; three stopped before drafting on nonexistent source indices. This improvement is promising operational evidence, not proof that the revision always makes better designs.

Across 32 streamed unit attempts, 22 produced accepted units. Eight earlier non-streaming transport failures remain separate. Including a tiny transport probe, the ledger records 79 Luna calls, 71 completed replies and eight calls with unknown usage. Known usage costs about $0.82 at published standard API rates. The conservative total is $4.78, including full reservations for unknown usage. Every prototype stayed far below its $30 theoretical ceiling. These amounts cover Luna execution, not the Astra implementation/review agents, and are not subscription invoices.

## What the experiment controls

Both tracks use the same frozen research record with 43 factual claims. The controlled baseline receives that same condensed research, so it is not an exact replay of the earlier default run with nine claims. The hidden track receives Luffy's name and research, without the preferred design. The brief track also receives long-range melee, Conqueror's/Observation/Armament Haki paths, and Gear forms unlocking attacks where supported. Missing a withheld preference is not instruction disobedience.

All prototypes use the same classic UnitSpec and unchanged validators. Evaluation compiles 28 representative builds and runs six scenarios when the candidate supports them. A separate stationary enemy at distance 50 probes reach. That number is an experimental probe, not the definition of long-range melee. Actual attack delivery still matters.

The initial eight non-streaming runs all failed around 126 seconds with HTTP 524 and returned no draft. A small transport probe worked; streamed full drafts then completed beyond the gateway cutoff. Those eight attempts remain transport failures with unknown usage, not evidence about design quality. The comparison uses fresh streamed runs and retains their full outcomes separately.

Initial builders used Astra Medium; the compiler, staged, patch and combined builders used Astra High. All execution, including planning, critique and repair, stayed on Luna. An independent Astra High reviewer saw opaque labels, source material, units and execution evidence without pipeline identities, prompts, costs or reasoning settings. It inspected returned adaptation notes separately after reviewing the units. A fresh final reviewer independently compared the final revision against selected earlier candidates, again without identities or settings. This is model-assisted review, not a human playtest.

## What changed in the combined workflow

The combined pipeline begins with a compact feasibility and source-allocation plan. It receives explicit claim numbers, effective cost/range/complexity limits and actual scheduling semantics. It records supported requests, adaptations and omissions before drafting. It then produces the full DSL, runs mechanical diagnostics, and proposes bounded atomic edits. A rejected edit or failed review preserves the previous draft. At most four calls are allowed, including one final repair.

The final `refined` revision bounds claim indices in the planning schema and permits one targeted planning correction. It also supplies the exact mutable upgrade parameters from the real schema. This addresses invalid citation IDs and the repeated confusion between projectile speed, shot count and pierce. It uses three calls normally and five at most. The original combined implementation stays frozen for comparison.

This retains the full DSL's forms, resources, states and other supported mechanics. The separate compact compiler is faster and structurally reliable in this study, but its smaller language excludes those capabilities. Validating the compact compiler's output does not establish parity with the full generator.

Feasibility plans remain model judgments. One medium plan overcounted its own mechanism allocation and received an explicit budget warning. The final draft fit the real limit. Some plans also promised secondary on-hit actions or targeting behavior that became different operations in the final unit. Keeping the plan makes these discrepancies reviewable; it does not certify the result.

## What the Luffy outputs show

The combined medium brief candidate is a useful accepted partial adaptation. Its base Pistol is a direct strike with range 60. It has three Haki paths and Gear Second, Fourth and Fifth encounter forms that each enable an additional attack. Independent form probes observed additional damage from those unlocked attacks. Gear Third is an upgrade attack. Gear Fourth variants and runtime switching are explicitly omitted. Its atomic review proposed an incorrect repair, which was rejected, preserving the valid draft.

The hidden medium candidate is much weaker. It uses Rubber Reach, Gears and Armament, and Observation and Liberation paths, with no forms. An upgrade promises projectile speed 36 but changes neither speed nor effective behavior. A later upgrade promises speed 44 while speed stays 28. This is a concrete prose/mechanics failure despite passing validators. The independent review also found disagreement between its plan and implementation.

The compact compiler's high brief candidate implements three substantive Haki branches and actual distant direct melee. Its xhigh brief candidate better distinguishes Gear Second's speed, Third's prepared heavy hit, Fourth's compressed line strike and Fifth's zone attack. Both omit forms through the compiler's narrower capability set. Their returned omission notes explain the compromise; it still leaves the form requirement unmet.

Additional planning and critique did not consistently produce better content. The standalone plan pipeline failed one brief attempt before drafting because its selected plan IDs disagreed. The staged pipeline failed both high attempts on fragment ownership and shared writes. The full-revision critique pipeline produced a richer hidden unit, but its stamina unlock had no reachable consumer in a tier-two crosspath. The atomic-patch hidden candidate moved the spender within crosspath reach and retained an attack-unlocking Fifth form. These are useful comparisons that a scalar score would hide.

The refined medium brief run shows why better acceptance is insufficient. It has working forms and long reach, but all attacks are projectiles despite its melee description. Its returned notes also call two forms upgrade-granted even though they are externally unlocked. The high brief run does use direct and line strikes with three form attack additions and honest generic Gear Fourth limits. Extra reasoning improved that particular artifact, at nearly three times the runtime.

The refined hidden medium candidate has an upgrade that improves a still-disabled Haki action before its tier-two unlock. Its emitter-count upgrade runs under aggregate scheduling and shows no measured gain. The high hidden candidate has cleaner progression and more distinct Gear attacks, but groups Haki into one branch and omits forms. It remains an alternative adaptation of the hidden prompt.

## Evaluation changes worth keeping

Validity, adaptation fidelity, execution usefulness and cost need separate judgments. The public diagnostic report already removed the misleading quality total. This experiment adds exact scheduling inspection and compiled form action inventories without changing those diagnostic weights.

A recurring primary action uses `max(trigger.intervalSeconds, timing.cooldownSeconds)`. The direct pipeline produced three distinct upgrade/action changes where lower cooldown did not shorten that scheduled period. A real simulation regression proves the mechanism: with a two-second trigger, cooldown 2 to 1 still produces six hits over ten seconds; with a 0.05-second trigger floor, it produces eleven. Windup and other mechanics still affect resolved attacks. A masked cooldown does not make every part of an upgrade useless.

A separate suspected targeting-cap rule was withdrawn after inspecting the simulator. Targeting bounds primary aims; projectile pierce can add collateral targets. A pierce upgrade cannot be dismissed merely because the primary target count is lower. Existing scenario observations remain valid within their stated coverage.

The final reviewer also found aggregate-rate emitter upgrades with zero gain and manual-action cooldown changes masked by an unchanged ability cooldown. These need their own scoped checks; the current scheduling inspector only diagnoses recurring primary intervals. Conversely, paired form simulations show real added damage where a static diagnostic called the actions disconnected. Executed evidence takes precedence over that weak static label. Scenario gaps and diagnostic false positives remain visible.

Source attribution needs semantic review. Several low, medium and xhigh compact-compiler outputs pointed to valid records but cited the wrong facts. Internal Destruction sometimes cited Future Sight or poison immunity. Explicit numbering helps prevent indexing mistakes, but correct citation syntax does not prove that a source supports an adaptation. The xhigh brief output had substantially better mappings than those earlier examples.

BTD6 remains the gameplay baseline discussed in [the earlier capture review](luffy-btd6-review.md). Useful numerical upgrades belong in that baseline. Scoring every tier for novelty would punish legitimate tower designs. The captured BTD6 units have not yet been translated into a qualified executable corpus, so no general scorer calibration or balance claim follows from this study.

## Recommended direction

Use the refined combined full-DSL workflow as an opt-in starting point for richer workshop drafts that need forms and explicit adaptation notes, with source and prose review before accepting a design. Keep the compact compiler as a fast option for its documented subset. A source-specific brief materially improves alignment with the intended design; the hidden track measures what the generator chooses without that guidance.

The complete Gear workflow requires a ruleset decision. The unchanged default counts every declared form and ability toward three prominent mechanics, plus control and damage-over-time categories. It cannot fit five independent Gear declarations. Existing forms are external encounter selections, not runtime modes. Supporting combat transformations needs activation, duration, reversion, mutual exclusion and executable transitions. Increasing the count alone does not implement them.

Before changing the default pipeline or tuning diagnostic weights, test unrelated characters and game-unit concepts with independent judgments, then validate a narrow BTD6 translator against captured pure-path and crosspath builds. Keep tower families used for development out of holdout evaluation. Luffy is now a development case, not a generalization test.

## Retained artifacts and verification

The repository contains prototype source, common research, compact candidate artifacts, original freeze records, cost ledger and outcome tables. Original prompts, provider replies, intermediate drafts, full simulations and opaque review packets remain under `.scratch/pipeline-prototypes-20260908/`. The checked-in code is formatted and uses equivalent source-stripping code for lint compliance; the original frozen local files are preserved. Research JSON has the same content with a different formatting hash, both recorded.

Offline checks passed for 47 tests plus five staged execution scenarios. They exercise real core execution and validation with fake model responses, including atomic rollback, call limits, source isolation, form preservation and cadence behavior. The accepted combined medium and refined high brief results also passed the actual CLI validator through their checked-in trusted definitions. The local HTTP provider tests also verify that a command-provider setting cannot bypass the Luna-only execution policy. The development server stayed stopped. No Foundation change was needed for the experiment.

# W packet review

Units were read before their returned adaptation notes. This review remains blind to identities and settings. The source and limits are the same as `existing-review.md`.

## W31, hidden track

W31 is mechanically accepted, with a small and understandable kit: heavier long-reach projectiles, manual Conqueror's control, and a cadence/volley path with late reveal. Its base range is 30 and Elastic Arsenal extends it to 44. No attack is direct or line delivery, no Gear form exists, and the named paths do not match the hidden three-Haki preference. All 28 distance-50 probes deal zero damage. This establishes a reach bound, not a universal failure of ranged-punch fidelity.

The simple kit loses several major source distinctions. Armament, Emission, Internal Destruction, Gear III and Gear IV attacks, and awakened Gear V behavior have no substantive implementation. Observation is only a reveal added to existing hits. The returned omissions admit this narrowing. They sometimes cite the absence of runtime transformations to justify dropping even supported attack adaptations, which is a design choice rather than a full-contract impossibility. The King Kong Impact capstone is a damage addition with a name absent from the captured source catalog. The numeric upgrade is legitimate; the source does not substantiate that specific named attack.

Haki tier 1 changes a locked action before tier 2 makes it usable. This costs 110 credits for no immediate behavior, and all six diagnostics report zero change. Tier 3 and tier 5 reduce the action cooldown to 10 and 7 seconds, but the granted ability retains a 12-second cooldown and recharge. All six diagnostics report zero gain for both transitions, including no additional status uptime. The promise of a shorter usable Haki cooldown is therefore unsupported by the evidence. Tier 4 does increase support status uptime by 1.5 target-seconds and burst uptime by 0.5. This is a good example of a useful numeric control upgrade beside ineffective ones.

Captain's Momentum tier 2 promises two attack streams by adding emitterCount under aggregate timing. The six scenarios record identical damage, hits, failures and other measured results before and after. A name and a compiled emitter count do not establish a second functional stream. Tier 3 adds projectilesPerCycle and has a clearer path to actual extra hits. The tier-5 reveal capstone has zero sampled benefit; that alone does not prove reveal is universally useless. The aura has no weak-willed filter, while source claim 32 restricts its knockout to weak-willed people or animals. The returned note defers resistance to external context without implementing that qualifier.

One ability and the control category consume two prominent mechanics. No resource is declared, so there is no resource unlock-to-consumer gap. The diagnostics flag 9 zero-gain and 5 low-gain transitions, no regressing transitions, and 6 dominated build pairs. These include crosspath versions of the unavailable Haki tier-1 investment. Complete selections and deltas are retained in `w-evidence.json`.

Compared with hidden U94, W31 has a clearer manual-control job and avoids U94's false projectile-speed upgrade. U94 better preserves Gear-specific attacks, a stamina tradeoff, short Emission and weak-willed filtering. Both miss the hidden Haki-path organization and external forms. I prefer U94 for source coverage and W31 for the legibility of the three broad jobs. I cannot prefer W31 for overall progression quality while its first Haki purchase and two cooldown upgrades have no observed effect. This is a dimension-specific comparison of two samples, not a reliable pipeline ranking.

## W86, brief track

W86 implements real Gear II, IV and V encounter forms. Gear II adds a two-projectile 0.45-second attack; Gear IV adds a slower 58-damage piercing projectile; Gear V adds a 45-damage attack with nearest targeting and two acquired targets. Their durable form probes add 2412, 1624 and 1710 damage respectively. The forms clearly do more than rename a build. Gear II's barrage is supported by actual shots and cadence. Three forms consume the full prominent-mechanic budget, with no ability, control or DoT category.

W86 has genuine distant reach. Its 70-unit base projectile deals 352 damage in the 10-second distance-50 probe, with first effect at 0.516667 seconds, consistent with 0.1 windup plus travel over 50 at speed 120. All attacks use projectile delivery. The final placement note openly discloses this adaptation, so the unit supports an elastic ranged-punch theme while failing to supply the direct/line melee behavior sought by the rubric. U61's direct-strike base expresses that part of the request more closely.

The three branches have distinct property ownership, but weaker source and player-job differences than their labels suggest. Conqueror's and Armament both mostly add damage to the same projectile. Observation adds range, cooldown reductions and extra shots; its numeric cadence progression is real because the 0.15 trigger floor remains below cooldown. Armament's tier-3 form grant gives it a distinct milestone. Emission is extra damage on each long-range punch, not a separately ranged short blast, and Internal Destruction is more damage without penetration. The notes disclose both reductions. Conqueror's knockout and Observation sensing/prediction are omitted. Observation can still be a useful firing-rate path without literal prediction.

The Conqueror's tier-3 'King's Barrage' changes emitterCount under aggregate timing. All six scenarios report zero change in hits, damage, failures or utility. This is the same observed emitter problem as W31. Later Observation shot-count upgrades are mechanically different and should not receive that criticism merely because both are called barrages. Gear Third is completely absent as an attack or form. The final summary candidly calls it an unsupported distinction; the returned request treatment overclaims that Gear Third and Gear Fourth variants are represented through named upgrades. No such upgrade descriptions exist.

The returned form notes are also stale. They describe Gear II and V as locked and granted by owning upgrades, but `/forms/0/externallyUnlocked` and `/forms/2/externallyUnlocked` are true, and no node grants either. Their paired probes compile them with zero upgrades. This is a real option available at purchase, not earned Gear progression. The same notes describe combat differences as coming only from paths, overlooking the actual extra attacks. Gear IV alone is granted by Armament tier 3. Base plus externally selectable Gear II/V may be a reasonable configuration choice, but it should be stated accurately.

The omission of Gear IV variants is honest and supported by the incomplete source catalog and available form budget. The note claiming the contract lacks an aura mechanic is too broad: W31 and the existing brief samples have aura delivery. Omitting knockout to fit three forms is a defensible budget choice. It is not proof the DSL cannot express bounded control.

W86 passes the supplied validity checks. Diagnostics flag 1 zero-gain, 11 low-gain and 3 regressing transitions, with 8 dominated pairs. For example, Observation tier 5 costs 2000 and dominates Armament tier 5 with Gear IV at 2130 in the six scenarios. This does not establish global dominance, and freely available Gear II/V configurations are not exhaustively crossed with every path in these comparisons.

Within the brief track I prefer U61 for direct melee delivery, Gear III attack coverage and accurate form progression. I prefer W86 over V68 for candidly omitting Gear IV variants rather than claiming nonexistent configurations. V68 better separates short Emission and gives earned Gear II/V unlocks. S18 and T63 retain clearer manual-control, awareness and line-damage jobs, while W86 fulfills more of the external-form request. W86 therefore adds useful form behavior without resolving the whole brief or producing a clear overall content winner.

## W57, hidden track

W57 is a more complete and more readable attack adaptation than W31. Elastic Rush develops a 30-to-38-range projectile through real 0.80-to-0.68-to-0.58 cooldown changes, two shots per cycle, and collateral coverage. Haki Command adds Armament damage, a 24-range line Emission, and weak-willed-only manual control. Gears as Attacks adds Gear III and IV direct strikes at 18 and 26 range, then a 32-range chain attack for Gear V. The attacks have different periods, windups and effects on groups. Numeric damage upgrades strengthen those existing jobs without needing new operations at every tier.

The unit passes all supplied validity checks. It has no forms, one manual ability, and the control category, totaling two prominent mechanics. No resource is declared. It fails all distance-50 probes, and its direct/line attacks are shorter than the 30-unit base. It does not recover the hidden preference for distant direct/line melee, three Haki paths, or external Gear forms. Observation and Future Sight are entirely omitted. These are hidden-preference and source-coverage gaps, not failures to obey an unseen brief.

Its source treatment is mostly candid. Internal Destruction only increases damage; the summary explicitly says it does not simulate penetration. Gear II is cadence rather than a form, Gear III/IV are heavier separate attacks, and Gear V is a chain hit rather than environmental rubberization. The chain is a design choice inspired by claim 24's increased freedom, not a specific behavior established by that claim. 'Gum-Gum Gigant' and 'Compressed Kong Gun' are not substantiated names in the captured factual catalog. The broad enlarged/compressed attack families are substantiated by claims 19 and 21-22. Returned notes should distinguish their named inventions or outside knowledge from the source-supported attack concepts.

Conqueror's has the source-faithful weak-willed tag, unlike W31's unfiltered aura. Its Haki tier-4 grant adds no damage, hits or status uptime in the supplied scenarios, while producing two more support targeting failures and one more burst failure. That yields the one reported regressing edge. It does not prove the ability is useless against weak-willed enemies; the supplied evidence has not demonstrated a successful tagged-target cast. The ability's declared 30-second cooldown is clearly separate from the action's 0.15 floor. Unlike W31, no upgrade promises to improve that cooldown while leaving recharge unchanged.

No zero-gain edge is reported. There are 7 low-gain edges, 1 regressing edge, and 1 dominated pair. Elastic Rush tier 5 plus Haki tier 1 at 2250 dominates Elastic Rush tier 5 plus Gears tier 1 at 2260 in the six scenarios. The first range purchase is low gain in those scenarios, but its 30-to-38 reach is a real capability. The exact edges and scenario facts remain in `w-evidence.json`.

I prefer W57 to W31 for source coverage, meaningful purchase progression and copy-mechanic correspondence. It preserves Gear III/IV/V attack distinctions and avoids the unavailable Haki tier-1 purchase and ineffective emitter/cooldown promises. Against U94, W57 has cleaner progression and a distinct Gear V chain attack; U94 retains Observation reveal and explicit stamina consumption. I prefer W57 for progression and prose accuracy, with U94 ahead on those two source dimensions. None of the three hidden samples supplies all three desired Haki branches or any external Gear forms. This is evidence about these particular outputs, with one sample each.

## W24, brief track

W24 is the most convincing W sample for the explicit brief. It combines direct elastic melee, the three Haki paths, earned Gear II/IV/V form unlocks, and clear descriptions of what the forms actually do. The base direct strike reaches 36, rising through Observation to 44 and 54. At distance 50, Observation tier 4 deals 196 damage in 14 hits and tier 5 deals 252 in 18 hits. Base failure at 50 is expected from its declared 36 range and does not erase its ranged direct-strike identity.

All three forms add characteristic attacks. The Gear II form raises durable damage from 560 to 968, Gear IV from 624 to 1200, and Gear V from 1062.5 to 1958.5. Gear II attacks every 0.9 seconds after 0.05 windup, Gear IV every 2.6 after 0.2, and Gear V every 1.1 after 0.1. These are additive external configurations with no runtime switching or reversion. The source's Gear II speed and Gear IV compressed force have recognizably different attacks. Gear V remains a modest source adaptation through strength and a longer reach, with rubberization explicitly omitted. Exactly three forms consume the three prominent slots, and there are no active abilities, control or DoT categories.

W24 avoids the ineffective aggregate-emitter promise seen in W31 and W86. Its primary action uses per-emitter timing. The form-free Observation tier-3 build records 40 Gum-Gum Pistol hits and 560 damage over 30 seconds at a 1.5-second period, supporting its second independently resolved strike. The same tier's Gear II grant separately adds 34 hits and 408 damage. Cadence, emitter count and form benefit therefore have concrete, distinct execution evidence.

The paths remain mostly damage specializations. Conqueror's layers per-hit damage, Observation develops reach and cadence, and Armament adds a short 18-to-28-range line attack followed by per-hit damage. That is enough for different reach and rate decisions, but S18/T63 offer stronger control-versus-awareness-versus-line-coverage jobs. W24's Gear Third is an added 22-damage effect on the ordinary punch, later raised to 36. It adds no slower timing, larger geometry, new attack or form. The source supports high damage from enlargement, so this is a thin but honestly described adaptation. The connection between Gear III and the Conqueror branch is a design allocation, not a source-established Haki dependency.

The source notes are substantially closer to the final unit than U61, V68 or W86's notes. They correctly distinguish base, the three actual forms, Gear Third damage, generic Gear Fourth and omitted variants. They accurately disclose no weak-willed knockout, literal Future Sight, defensive Haki, internal penetration or environmental rubberization. A minor stale statement says a legal build requires a primary path through tier 5, while the supplied base and partial-path probes are also legal. That does not undermine the final gameplay claims. The notes also list Emission cooldown ownership without any final cooldown upgrade, but do not promise a specific missing change in a node.

W24 passes schema, semantics, default profile, request and sampled compilation checks. It has 1 zero-gain transition, 8 low-gain transitions, no regressing transition, and 1 dominated pair in the six diagnostics. The zero-gain transition is Armament tier 3's reach increase from 18 to 28. This is a genuine range change that the six scenarios do not reward, not a no-op operation. Observation tier 5 plus Conqueror tier 1 dominates Observation tier 5 plus Armament tier 1 under the evaluator's cost and node-count comparison. Exact selections and scenario facts are in `w-evidence.json`.

I prefer W24 to W86 for explicit-brief fit, functioning emitter progression and accurate form descriptions. I prefer W24 to V68 for truthful handling of Gear IV variants and actual rather than claimed targeting changes. Compared with U61, W24 is the cleaner candidate for further development, with fewer misleading adaptation claims and more convincing progression evidence. U61 still covers more of the literal requested fantasy: direct melee at 60 from purchase, a separate slower Gear III attack, and Observation reveal. W24 starts at 36, reaches 54 later, and reduces Gear III to more damage. These are different strengths; the review does not collapse them into one score.

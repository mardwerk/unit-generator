# Existing comparisons, before W packets

This review uses the supplied rubric, captured source, blinded unit packets and then each unit's returned decision notes. No pipeline identity is inferred. Claim numbers below are one-based, so returned `claimIndices` require adding one. This is a content review without an overall score. Numeric damage and cadence upgrades can serve a clear job.

All five units pass the supplied schema, semantics, default profile, request checks and sampled compilation. Those checks do not establish source fidelity. None invents Gear First. The base state is the unformed or starting build. The shared captured article supports elastic ranged punches in claims 11-12, speed and strain in 17-18, enlarged heavy blows in 19-20, compression and Armament in 21-22, awakened rubberization in 24-27, and three Haki families in 29-36. It does not supply a Gear Fourth variant catalog. A source citation beside a variant name cannot fill that gap.

U61 most directly satisfies the explicit reach and external-form request among these packets. Its base direct strike reaches 60, deals 90 per hit every 1.2 seconds after 0.1 windup, and deals 810 damage in the 10-second distance-50 probe. Three actual forms enable distinct extra attacks. In the durable form comparisons, Gear Fifth raises damage 5645 to 8395, Gear Second 3060 to 7995, and Gear Fourth 6950 to 11150. This is executable form benefit, despite diagnostic prose calling these effects disconnected. The forms are additive encounter configurations. They do not replace the base attack, create runtime transformations or implement arbitrary awakening behavior.

U61 keeps Haki paths distinct from those forms. Conqueror's develops coated damage and collateral coverage, Observation has cadence and reveal, and Armament adds separate heavy attacks. Conqueror's unconsciousness is explicitly omitted to preserve the three-mechanic budget. Three forms consume that budget; there are no abilities, control statuses or DoT. Gear Third is only an attack unlocked by Armament tier 4, and Gear Fourth variants remain absent. These omissions are candid and leave part of the brief unmet. Gear Fifth's finite damage burst captures increased strength, not the source's environmental rubberization. Internal Destruction is ordinary damage with an `internal-haki` type, not demonstrated armor penetration. Emission's 48 reach is shorter than the 60 base, but calling it a distant blast stretches the source's short-blast identity.

U61's returned plan is stale in several concrete places. Observation tier 1 promises improved priority, but changes cooldown only. Conqueror's pressure is described as triggered by the primary hit, but the final action is independently scheduled every 3.5 seconds. Future Sight's final operation extends reveal from 6 to 10 seconds on a 4-second repeat; it does not alter priority or establish literal prediction. The reveal duration upgrade produces zero measured benefit in all six supplied scenarios. Its Gear Second 'Barrage' is a single emission at a faster 0.65 period, not a multi-hit volley. That name is weaker than its accurate summary of faster strikes.

V68 implements three real Gear II/IV/V forms, with durable damage increases of 816 to 2411, 2852 to 4232 and 2312 to 4216 in their paired probes. It has a 32-unit base direct strike, rising through Observation to 40, 50 and 64. At distance 50, Observation tier 3 produces 336 damage, tier 4 384 and tier 5 480. Zero base damage at that distance does not disprove meaningful elastic melee reach at 32. Observation has a useful numeric reach-and-cadence job even without a novel effect. Armament's 18-unit Emission preserves the source's short projected blast better than the other explicit-brief comparisons.

V68's major defect is invented completeness. `/forms/1/summary` and `/actions/5/summary` describe Boundman, Snakeman and Tankman encounter configurations. There is one Gear Fourth declaration and one fixed enabled attack; no variant parameters, selections or different operations exist. The returned note also claims these three configurations as implemented, citing claims 21-22, which do not enumerate them. Observation tier 2 promises a target-priority improvement but only grants Gear Second, while the base attack already targets first. Returned notes promise a delayed Conqueror's tier-4 burst and Future Sight selection changes; the final nodes only add damage and multiply cooldown. Conqueror's coating damage is defensible under claim 36, but unconsciousness and literal Armament defense negation remain absent. Three forms fill the prominent-mechanic budget. Gear Third remains an attack rather than a form.

S18 gives the clearest straightforward path jobs. Observation buys a revealing direct strike, then reach and faster cadence. Armament buys a line strike, larger impact and more collateral pierce. Conqueror's buys a manually timed stun plus coated and awakened attacks with actual two-shot and three-shot upgrades. Internal Destruction's pierce is a disclosed approximation, but passing through several enemies differs from destroying one target from within in claim 35. Gear Second and Gear Third mostly rename useful numeric changes to existing attacks; Gear Fourth also adds damage rather than unlocking a configuration. Gear Fifth adds a distinct attack. There are no forms. One ability plus the control category counts as two prominent mechanics.

S18's `/actions/3/summary` and ability summary say 'weak-willed enemies', but includeTags is empty. This broadens claim 32 to all eligible nearby enemies. Returned capabilities disclose that tag filters are unavailable in this representation; the final copy should disclose the broadening too. Observation reaches 60 at tier 5 and deals 480 damage in the distance-50 probe. Its base 30 reach is not rejected solely for failing that probe. The form omission is honest about this output's reduced capabilities, but 'forms are unsupported' must not be mistaken for a limitation of the full default contract. U61 and V68 demonstrate supported external forms.

T63 has a stronger attack catalog than S18 for Gear identity. Gear Second is a separate 0.7-second direct strike, Gear Third a slower 2.4-second heavy blow, Gear Fourth a 3.2-second line strike, and Gear Fifth a zone hit. Observation adds homing and reveal, Conqueror's adds manual stun, and Armament builds heavy line damage. These are observable delivery and timing distinctions. The unit has zero external forms, so Gear attack unlocks do not fulfill the request for form-owned attacks. Maximum reach is 48, and all 28 distance-50 samples fail to hit. That bounds reach rather than supplying a universal long-range cutoff. The manual aura mechanically stuns all eligible targets; the source only supports unconsciousness of weak-willed targets. Its omission notes disclose form and resource limitations and do not claim literal defense negation. One ability and control category consume two prominent mechanics.

U94 is the sole existing hidden-track comparison. It independently groups rubber, combined Haki and Gear techniques, so it does not recover the hidden desired three-Haki-path organization. That is a preference mismatch, not disobedience to an unseen brief. Its base ranged punch is a projectile at 24, extending to 30; the only direct strike is Gear Third at 18, shorter than base range. No distance-50 sample hits. It therefore does not demonstrate long-range direct or line melee, though it does represent the ranged-punch source trait. Its weak-willed-tagged automatic Conqueror's aura is more faithful to claim 32 than S18 or T63's unfiltered control. The six scenarios do not establish its usefulness against a tagged population. Stamina is present from base with a base consumer, so there is no unlock-to-consumer gap. Gear attacks consume the same resource; costs and regeneration are explicit design adaptations rather than source quantities.

U94 has an unequivocal copy-mechanic contradiction. `rubber-arsenal-3`, Slingshot Velocity, promises speed 34 to 48 but sets existing projectiles-per-cycle and pierce to 1. Projectile speed stays 34. It produces zero measured utility gain. Future Sight reveal also has zero measured gain in the six supplied scenarios, which is weaker evidence of a defect because relevant hidden-target behavior may be absent. Gear 2's cooldown change really improves cadence because the trigger floor is only 0.15. The source-distinct internal penetration, runtime Gears and Gear Fifth awakening remain explicitly omitted. Awakened Freedom only multiplies Gear III/IV damage by 1.5. That is a useful damage upgrade but a thin Gear Fifth adaptation.

The retained `existing-evidence.json` records every flagged transition, its concrete parent and child builds, per-scenario deltas, and dominance pairs. Across support, grouped, fast, burst, durable and armoured: U61 has 1 zero-gain, 11 low-gain and 3 regressing transitions, with 7 dominated pairs. V68 has 0, 7 and 2, with no detected dominance. U94 has 2, 4 and 4, with 6 dominated pairs. S18 has 1, 7 and 1, with none. T63 has 0, 7 and 3, with none. The evaluator compares equal node counts and costs within 25 percent. These are scoped utility diagnostics, not global upgrade-worth labels. For example, U61's collateral upgrade adds no damage in the sampled grouped fight and creates three more failed acquisitions after enemies die; that is not evidence that collateral pierce is mechanically capped by acquisition count. S18's Armament tier-5 pierce has zero measured gain; T63's equivalent upgrade has equal damage with changed hit/failure counts. Both may matter against a different group.

Within the brief track I prefer U61 for actual form support, reach at purchase and candid omissions. V68 has useful cheaper-stage form unlocks and more range progression, but its claims of separate Gear Fourth configurations are false. I prefer S18 or T63 over either for the clarity of control-versus-cadence-versus-line-damage choices, with a tradeoff between S18's actual volleys and T63's separate Gear attacks and delivery types. Their lack of forms prevents a single unqualified preference for fulfilling the whole brief. These five outputs cannot establish pipeline reliability, general balance, or consistent preference across repeats. Cost, latency and failed-run accounting are outside the blinded content review.

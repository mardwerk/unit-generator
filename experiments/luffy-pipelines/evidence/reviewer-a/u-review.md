# U packet review, provisional

Unit mechanics were inspected before returned feasibility notes. Notes are checked against the actual output, not treated as independent certification. Labels remain opaque. Cross-replicate comparisons are descriptive within a track; no method identities, costs or effort settings are known.

## U28, hidden track

Validity is supported: all supplied sampled checks pass. Source fidelity is partial. Primary `/unit/actions/0` is first-target projectile range 20 equal to base, speed 28, damage 3, cooldown 1.1 and zero windup. Reach extends to 38; damage/cadence eventually reach 14/0.5. This is a plausible stretched-punch visual adaptation but no direct/line melee, and all 28 distant probes produce zero damage. Armament and Gears are numerical offense upgrades; Observation is on-hit reveal plus a manual reveal ability. Conqueror incapacitation, distinct Third/Fourth attacks, Fifth and forms are absent. In the hidden track, these are coverage losses and an alternative organization rather than instruction disobedience.

The three jobs at `/unit/upgradeGraph/paths` are reach/collateral, damage/cadence and information support. Numerical upgrades are not inherently inferior to new mechanics. Here, however, prose and execution diverge exactly. Node 1, Slingshot Speed, promises speed 36 and the plan agrees, but its two operations merely set projectile count to its existing one and range to the existing 25. `/unit/actions/0/delivery/projectileSpeedWorldUnitsPerSecond` stays 28. That purchase is a declared no-op, and `/evidence/diagnostics/reviewFindings/3` shows zero gain. Node 4 promises speed 44 yet only sets range 38 and re-sets collateral cap two. No operation changes speed anywhere. This is a supported correspondence failure, not a subjective source judgment.

Observation action 1 is manual, first-target aura with range 25 later 32, radius 8, one selected/delivered target, 12-second recovery and reveal 3 later 5 seconds. Node 12 grants the ability and evaluator findings record its hits, so the manual unlock is real. Revealing nearby enemies around a chosen distant target is not demonstrated: neither area target selection nor a radial effect trace is supplied, and aura geometry is unmodeled. Primary on-hit reveal duration grows from 2 to 10 seconds. Four of the five primary Observation purchases show zero measured utility; ability unlock adds hits but no damage/status uptime benefit in support/burst. A reveal-oriented job needs an appropriate concealment scenario before its usefulness can be established. The final unit has no discrete damaging burst mechanic despite its burst role and 'burst path' wording. There are no resources.

The returned plan in `review-decisions/U28.json`, `/feasibility/plan`, uses correct broad source indices and explicitly omits awakening, special Gear states, advanced Haki behaviors and resistances. It admits that external forms exist but chooses no form. That is honest lossy adaptation rather than impossibility of the full DSL. Some plan wording nevertheless says form-specific attack behavior is unsupported, which is too broad given other accepted outputs. The source has insufficient exact variant detail, but does establish enlargement/compression and permits design abstractions of them.

Plan-to-final correspondence is partial. The plan's Observation tier 3 prioritizes revealed targets; the final node grants a manual ability and every attack still uses first targeting. Planned final-tier on-hit radial reveal becomes a manual aura while the primary only lengthens single-target reveal. The speed changes are retained in final prose but disappear from operations. Source citations alone cannot make those promised behaviors executable. Traceability is consequently partial despite improved claim indexing. Feasibility is supported for accepted declared mechanics, but no-op upgrades and stale plan claims need repair.

Upgrade usefulness is partial: the exact no-op speed purchase and many reveal purchases have zero gain, several reach purchases add a single fast hit, and zero of 45 comparable crosspaths show strict dominance. Those facts are preserved in the mechanical findings, without treating lack of dominance as proof of quality. Compared with hidden S24, this has weaker direct melee evidence and a broken speed purchase; compared with S45/R73 it loses the executed form-unlocked attack. The manual information job might be useful in concealment scenarios, but the packet does not show that benefit.

## U61, brief track

This is the first reviewed accepted brief artifact that combines real distant direct melee, three Haki paths and multiple executable external forms. It still does not implement every requested form distinction. Validity is supported across all supplied checks, separately from its incomplete source fidelity and untested balance.

Primary `/unit/actions/0` is a strongest-target direct strike, range 60 equal to base, damage 90, cooldown 1.2 and 0.1 windup. Armament tier 1 extends it to 68. `/evidence/distantTargetProbe/0/report/damageByAction/gum-gum-pistol` records 810 damage at 50 before upgrades. This is direct melee-style reach, not a projectile relabeled as melee. Source numeric ranges are unspecified; 60 is acknowledged as a design choice.

Three Haki families have substantive though incomplete adaptations. Conqueror owns primary damage/collateral and a separate pressure strike, damage 180 later 280 every 3.5 seconds at range 60 with 0.2 windup. Its action summary explicitly adapts coating rather than claiming automatic knockout. This is source-backed attack enhancement, while weak-willed incapacitation is deliberately omitted to preserve the selected mechanic budget. Observation owns primary cadence and action 2, a recurring area-targeted direct reveal with cap eight, range 60, duration 6 later 10 and cooldown 4. That expresses presence detection but does not predict future motion. Armament adds range, Emission, Internal Destruction and Third. Emission action 3 does 140 damage every 4.5 seconds at range 48; Internal Destruction action 4 does 260 every 5.5 at range 60. Both are independent strongest-target direct strikes. Their damage types do not implement defense bypass. The returned plan explicitly limits internal damage to a bounded abstraction.

The three forms are separate, externally activated encounter configurations that each enable a distinct attack. `/unit/forms/0` enables Second action 6 with first targeting, range 60, 105 damage, 0.65 cooldown and 0.05 windup. Form 1 enables Fourth action 7 with strongest targeting, range 65, 300 damage, 2.2 cooldown and 0.25 windup. Form 2 enables Fifth action 8 with two-target capacity, range 70, 250 damage, 2.8 cooldown and 0.2 windup. These are faster, compressed-heavy and broader attacks, not just form names.

Form probes verify executable unlocks. `/evidence/formProbes/0`, variants 0/1 simulation 0, show durable Fifth damage 8395/5645, a gain of 2750; Second probe 1 shows 7995/3060, a gain of 4935; Fourth probe 2 shows 11150/6950, a gain of 4200. The added action damage matches those gains. The forms do not replace the base attack or perform runtime transformations. Their external encounter semantics are accurately described. No manual ability or resource consumption is introduced.

Third is an Armament tier-4 action unlock, not a form. Its 360-damage strongest-target direct blow has cooldown 5 and 0.7 windup at range 60, meaningfully slower and more prepared than Second. The returned request-by-request report explicitly discloses this treatment and limits the declared form budget to Second/Fourth/Fifth. Fourth variants are absent, with both the source catalogue gap and form budget explained. No Gear First is invented. The unit capstone name 'Boundman Force' still invokes a variant name absent from the captured source, even while the form itself correctly says variants are not separately implemented. Fifth environmental rubberization is expressly omitted.

Source organization is supported for Haki paths and partial for the full form brief. This is a more complete honestly bounded implementation than accepted form-free brief candidates. It remains an adaptation with requested Third/variant form distinctions omitted, not a literal complete brief realization. Gameplay differentiation is partial: the paths vary damage cadence, information and independent heavy strikes, but almost every added action is offense-only and Conqueror has no control. The `control` role is not substantively established by reveal alone. High output is not evidence of more faithful Haki specialization.

Prose/mechanic and plan-to-final correspondence are partial. The final pressure action correctly says recurring, but the plan says it should trigger as a secondary impact on the primary hit. In the final unit pressure, Emission and Internal Destruction each run independent interval triggers, with empty state interactions and no on-hit link. Planned Observation tier 1 changes target priority; the final primary is already strongest-targeted and the upgrade only shortens cooldown. Planned Future Sight timing/ordering becomes longer reveal duration. These are concrete changes of design, not proof the final actions fail; the preserved planning report must not be cited as the final executable contract. Gear Second's 'Barrage' name has one emission per cycle; its speed gives repeated attacks over time, but there is no multi-hit barrage cycle. Preparation and cadence differences do execute.

Upgrade usefulness is partial. Observation's duration increase from 6 to 10 seconds occurs on a four-second reveal cycle and shows zero measured gain at finding 2. Under uninterrupted eligible targeting, the earlier reveal already overlaps its next application; lasting after missed cycles remains a possible benefit outside these tests. Seven of 45 comparable crosspath pairs are dominated. The candidate's high damage saturates grouped/fast scenarios: form variants often all reach fast's 600 total damage, even when action contribution changes. Those ceilings make small incremental gains hard to interpret. Base durability 1000 and damage scale 90 are much larger than many other artifacts, but passing numeric bands does not establish cross-unit balance. Do not prefer this candidate simply for larger simulated totals.

The returned plan's claim indices mostly correspond to the correct Haki/Gear facts. Its planning checker flags six prominent mechanics against a three-mechanic limit; the final artifact reduces the planned secondary-ability structure and passes the actual default checks. Preserve both observations. The planning warning is not final invalidity, and final acceptance does not retroactively prove the plan feasible. Overall traceability is partial: good source indexing and explicit omissions coexist with stale plan trigger/priority promises.

For the brief track, U61 is currently the most useful accepted example for studying how external form-unlocked attacks can coexist with direct reach and the requested Haki organization. S18/T63 remain useful simpler combat adaptations, but they omit forms because their available subset cannot emit them. This is a comparison of actual artifacts and selected capabilities, not an overall numeric ranking or proof of generation-method superiority. U61 still needs honest acknowledgment of its Third/variant losses, plan-to-final reconciliation and appropriate balance/role testing.

## U94, hidden track

Validity is supported for all supplied sampled checks. Source fidelity and organization are partial. The hidden-track paths are Rubber Arsenal, Haki Mastery and Gear Techniques, an alternative job-based organization rather than failure to follow an explicit Haki brief. Primary action 0 is first-target projectile, range 24, speed 34, damage 3, cooldown 1 and no windup, spending one stamina. The only direct Gear strike, action 4, reaches 18, shorter than base. Fourth's range-32 compressed attack is projectile delivery. All 28 distant probes do zero damage. This expresses ranged elastic punching and prepared enlarged/compressed hits, but not the withheld long-range melee preference.

The source connections are stronger than names alone in several places. Third action 4 has 12 damage, four-second cooldown and 0.4 windup; Fourth action 5 has 18 damage, six-second cooldown and 0.5 windup. Both consume stamina, 18 and 24 respectively, and later gain cadence/damage. A distinct short-range strongest-target beam expresses Emission; a three-second reveal attached to that beam approximates Observation. Conqueror action 3 is an eight-second aura, radius/range 14, six target capacity, 1.5-second stun and an actual `weak-willed` filter. This accurately preserves the source susceptibility condition, though the supplied scenarios do not demonstrate suitable targets. Fifth only multiplies Third/Fourth damage by 1.5; no form or new awakening capability exists. The returned omissions openly state that loss.

Prose correspondence is contradicted for a repeated defect. `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/2`, Slingshot Velocity, promises speed 34 to 48, but merely re-sets projectile count and collateral capacity to their existing value one. No operation changes projectile speed. Finding 2 reports zero measured utility. This is the same exact promise-to-operation failure as U28's speed purchase, now on a separate artifact. It is not a speculative bug or general objection to numerical upgrades.

Plan-to-final correspondence is partial. `review-decisions/U94.json`, `/feasibility/plan/paths/0/progression/4`, promises an on-hit secondary direct follow-through after 0.2 seconds. Final action 1 is an independent strongest-target projectile with a 4.5-second cooldown and 0.2 windup, enabled at Rubber tier 5. The plan's on-hit Armament supplement becomes an independent range-10 beam every 2.5 seconds. No primary-hit trigger remains. The plan's Gear 2 cost of five stamina per emission remains one in the final Pistol, and planned Gear tier-4 reductions to Third/Fourth costs 15/20 never occur; final node 13 changes only cooldowns while final prose correctly says same costs. The unit descriptions partly reconcile these choices, but the preserved plan still claims different behavior.

Stamina is genuinely connected to consumers from placement. `/unit/resources/0` starts at cap 100 and regenerates eight per second, and primary/Third/Fourth have declared action costs. There is no unlock-to-consumer gap. At highest Gear cadence, declared sustained demand would be about 12.29 stamina per second when all attacks can fire, exceeding regeneration; this is a real potential limiting resource, not merely a decorative meter. Actual scarcity under target/range restrictions requires scenario evidence. Do not substitute the plan's different costs when interpreting it.

Gameplay differentiation is partial: ranged coverage, short-range Haki control/detection and resource-costed heavy attacks are different jobs. The final separate Emission's short range produces substantial targeting failures. Findings 0, 6 and 7 report lower aggregate utility on its main/crosspath unlocks; these are not automatically reduced damage, since failure counts influence the diagnostic. The Conqueror unlock regresses in finding 5 with no demonstrated weak-willed targets; that is inadequate scenario coverage of the intended condition. Reveal has zero gain at finding 4. Six of 45 comparable crosspath pairs are dominated. All exact selections/facts are retained in the mechanical findings.

Feasibility is supported for the accepted declared mechanics, with honestly disclosed missing forms/resistances, while numeric balance and claimed knockouts on actual eligible enemies remain unassessed. Traceability uses the correct broad source indices, improving on the earlier T index errors, but stale speed, trigger and resource-cost promises weaken the final audit. Source-specific filtering and more attack types do not offset those concrete mismatches. Relative to U28, this has richer operational Gear/Conqueror content, yet it reproduces the exact speed-upgrade no-op and adds plan-to-final trigger/cost drift. It does not establish a consistent overall improvement over the earlier hidden exemplars.

## U76, hidden track replicate 2

`/unit` is null. `/execution/error` reports `invalid-feasibility-source` at feasibility stage because a feasibility item cites a nonexistent source claim. All validation stages are not-completed. This is a workflow/provenance failure before unit creation. It is neither transport failure nor a schema-invalid unit, and source fidelity, gameplay, upgrades and prose correspondence are unassessed. No content ranking against available units is warranted. The failure belongs in reliability accounting once identities are revealed, with no invented quality score.

U53 brief replicate 2 and V27 hidden replicate 2 supplied no unit. Both failed at `/execution/error` with `invalid-feasibility-source`, a nonexistent source claim in a feasibility item. These are workflow failures. Candidate validity and content remain unassessed. Together with U76, the U/V batch contains three such failures, two hidden and one brief.

U32, brief replicate 1, accepted. Source fidelity is strongest in its explicit refusal to invent Fourth variants. `/unit/summary`, `/forms/0`, `/forms/1`, and decision `/feasibility/plan/requests/5` distinguish generic Fourth/Fifth external forms from Second/Third attack upgrades. Second is a real cooldown change, 1.1 to .85 seconds, and Third adds 2 damage to the primary. This preserves less characteristic Gear behavior than U61 or V68, but states the loss clearly. The two forms enable different attacks, direct Fourth range 38, damage 10, cooldown 1.4, versus line Fifth range 46, damage 7, cooldown 1.0, two-target declarations. Form probes show durable damage 298 to 518 for Fourth and 112 to 322 for Fifth, with burst gains 60 and 56. No live transformation or variant functionality should be credited.

Its actual long-range melee is direct Pistol range 34 to 42 to 50, with cooldown .6 at Observation tier 5. The fixed 50-unit probe shows 48 damage at tier 4 and 68 at tier 5, up to 85 with Armament crosspath. Conqueror adds a stun status, .8 to 1.2 seconds, to the primary. Observation adds a separate recurring direct reveal at range 34, while Armament unlocks an independent recurring line attack at range 30 with an extra damage packet at tier 3. These are distinct jobs, although reveal cannot reach the distant enemies accessed by the upgraded primary. Diagnostics report seven low-gain edges and 9/45 dominated comparable pairs, not universal dominance. The distant probe is concrete evidence that the range upgrades matter outside the default six scenarios.

U32 correspondence defects remain. `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/0/summary` says weak-willed targets, but `/unit/actions/0/targeting/includeTags` is empty and no condition enforces that eligibility. The decision uncertainty acknowledges reliance on consuming-game context; the emitted unit does not itself implement it. Decision `/feasibility/plan/paths/1/progression/1` and `/mechanics/1/implementation` promise target-priority changes, but node 6 enables reveal and leaves primary targeting strongest. Decision `/feasibility/plan/paths/2/progression/1`, `/baseAttack/description`, and `/mechanics/4/implementation` promise conversion of primary delivery to line; node 11 instead enables independent Emission and the primary remains direct. That changes which upgrades and targets affect Emission. The final unit accurately describes the separate action. Broad source claim indices for Haki and Gears are correct, but the retained plan is stale. Feasibility and validity pass sampled checks. Costs and latency remain unassessed.

V68, brief replicate 2, accepted. The base direct Pistol reaches 32, then 40, 50 and 64. All seven attacks are direct strikes. Distinct attacks include Second damage 29/cooldown .55/windup .05/range 32; Third 72/2.4/.35/range 34; Fourth 60/1.35/.2/range 38; Fifth 68/1.1/.18/range 48 with two-target declarations. External Second, Fourth and Fifth forms enable their attacks without removing primary attacks. Third is an Armament upgrade action. Form probes prove additive durable gains of 1595 for Second, 1380 for Fourth and 1904 for Fifth, with burst gains 435, 360 and 544. The fixed distant probe reaches 680 damage in sampled upgraded builds. This is accepted long-range melee with distinguishable Gear timing, but not all requested forms or variants.

V68's three Haki paths have less functional separation than U32's. Conqueror is added damage and target-count pressure, with no knockout or control. Observation is primary range and cadence, with no reveal and no acquired targeting change. Armament adds damage and three independent recurring attacks, Third, Emission and Internal Destruction. Diagnostic evidence has seven low-gain edges and two regressions consisting of extra targeting failures without lost damage. There are 0/45 dominated comparable crosspath pairs in these scenarios, which does not establish balance. Do not infer a structural cap from targeting count alone.

V68 has a concrete prose defect at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/6/summary`: it promises prioritization of the first eligible enemy, but its only operation is grant-form, and `/unit/actions/0/targeting/type` is already first at base. The upgrade does provide a real form unlock, so the whole node is not useless. Decision `/feasibility/plan/paths/1/progression/3` and `/mechanics/4/implementation` promise Future Sight target selection changes; final node 8 only multiplies cooldown by .82. Decision `/feasibility/plan/paths/0/progression/3` promises a delayed secondary burst; final node 3 only adds 10 to a primary damage effect. Decision Internal Destruction says an additional effect on an eligible primary target, while `/unit/actions/4/trigger` schedules a separate strongest-target direct strike every 1.2 seconds.

The larger V68 fidelity/correspondence problem is Fourth variants. `/unit/actions/5/summary`, `/unit/forms/1/summary`, `/unit/requirements/visuals/3/description`, and decision `/feasibility/plan/mechanics/1/implementation` claim Boundman, Snakeman and Tankman encounter configurations. The form has exactly one enable-action operation for one fixed strike, with no separate variant declarations or properties. The captured source also does not establish those named variants. Decision uncertainties admit that source gap, which does not make the claimed configurations implemented or sourced. Third-as-upgrade and three-form budget limits are disclosed honestly. Broad claim indices for elasticity, Haki, Emission, Internal Destruction and Gear traits are appropriate. Sampled validity passes; cost and latency remain unassessed.

Within U/V, I prefer U61 when judging accepted brief coverage and demonstrated distant melee with distinct Gear attacks. U32 is preferable for implemented Conqueror control and honest generic Fourth treatment, but reduces Second/Third to scalar upgrades and has nine sampled dominated pairs. V68 provides good Gear timing differences and no sampled dominated pairs, but its nonexistent Fourth configurations and targeting claims make its prose less trustworthy. U61 itself retains plan drift and lacks Third-as-form and Fourth variants, so none is a complete brief implementation. In hidden U/V, U94 has more differentiated Gear/Haki actions and a connected resource system than U28, while U28 is simpler and has fewer sampled crosspath dominance findings. Both have exact promised projectile-speed changes absent from operations. Hidden-track omissions of the designer preference are coverage differences, not disobedience.

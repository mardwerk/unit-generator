## R29

The source organization is supported: `/unit/upgradeGraph/paths` separates Conqueror stun/chain, Observation reach/homing priority and Armament damage/Emission. Forms are separate and distinguish base, Second, Third, three Fourth adaptations and Fifth. Descriptions explicitly call the named Fourth variants design adaptations of incomplete generic source evidence. Runtime limitations and missing weaknesses are explained. That is better traceability than asserting source-unsupported variant facts without qualification.

Content execution is unassessed because `/evidence/diagnostics` is null and both probe lists are empty. `/evidence/validity/schemaAndSemantics` reports six attempts to disable already-disabled `gum-pistol`, at form indices 1 through 6. The attack is initially locked, with base form 0 enabling it; every Gear form starts by disabling it. The form lifecycle does not ensure the action is enabled when the disable operation runs. Runner `/execution/validation/constraints` additionally reports the requested complexity limit exceeded. The evaluator's empty profile/request arrays after semantic failure must not be read as a clean profile pass. This is failed output, not an honest refusal and not a viable form implementation.

Source fidelity and prose correspondence are partial. Primary `/unit/actions/0` is first-target projectile range 34, damage 12, cooldown 1.08 and no windup. Observation's action 2 is strongest-target homing projectile range 40, later 52. These do not implement the promised long-range melee. Armament's range-30 line strike and Third's range-36 line strike are physical/direct delivery alternatives, but neither has probe evidence at a distant target. The stated Third body enlargement is backed by a heavier 34-damage strike, longer cooldown 1.85 and 0.1 windup. Second speeds the projectile to cooldown 0.72. Fourth forms differ in speed, impact, targeting and range, and Fifth enables a five-target chain rather than copying Pistol. Those are declared meaningful distinctions, though lifecycle failure blocks executable confirmation.

Conqueror's chain action 1 applies damage plus 1.1-second stun, and the prose explicitly admits that there is no weak-willed classifier. Observation describes homing/strongest priority as an abstraction rather than literal future vision. Armament's Internal Destruction prose admits a damage increase instead of penetration. These limitations are candid. All major paths modify Pistol at early tiers, however, while each Gear form disables Pistol. If the lifecycle were repaired, those Pistol-only damage/range/stun purchases would not automatically transfer to Gear attacks. That is a declared compatibility risk, not a simulated regression. There are no resources. Upgrade utility and gameplay gains remain unassessed; the artifact has no numerical scenario evidence.

## S24

Source fidelity is partial, with supported elastic melee. `/unit/actions/0` uses direct-strike delivery, first targeting, base range 32, 24 blunt damage, 1.2-second cooldown and 0.15 windup. Armament tiers extend it to 40 then 52, and `/evidence/distantTargetProbe/10/report/damageByAction/a-0` records 558 damage at distance 50 with the five Armament nodes. This establishes real melee-style distant hits, without treating distance 50 as a universal quality threshold. Gear 2 Pump's cooldown upgrades descend to 0.32 while trigger interval remains 0.15, so they can change actual cadence. Numerical specialization is appropriate here; a new operation type at every tier is unnecessary.

Source organization is partial. The paths are Gear 2 Pump, Armament and Conqueror, not the withheld three-Haki preference. That is a reasonable hidden-track choice, not disobedience. Observation, Third, Fourth/variants and Fifth are absent; `/unit/forms` is empty. The unit provides no explanation for dropping the known Gear family, though the source's missing variant catalogue limits what exact Fourth names could have been supported. No Gear First is invented.

Gameplay jobs differ: speed increases sustained hits, Armament increases damage/reach/collateral capacity, and Conqueror adds a manual stun. `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/10` both enables and grants the ability, so the manual action is reachable. Its range starts 16, radius 16, windup 0.45, 2.5-second stun and 18-second recovery. The targeting list admits eight targets but delivery cap is one; the packet supplies no dedicated multi-enemy aura test, so broad crowd incapacitation is not assumed. The weak-willed description at `/unit/actions/1/summary` is not implemented by its empty filters. Internal Destruction at node 8 increases hit capacity from one to two, which is collateral penetration, not proven defense penetration or destruction inside a body. Emission is a range change on the original strike rather than a new emitted blast. These are partial adaptations with unacknowledged losses in prose.

Upgrade usefulness is partial. Conqueror tiers 3, 4 and 5 only change action range to 24, 28 and 32 while radius remains 16. All three edges have zero measured gain in `/evidence/diagnostics/reviewFindings/1` through `/3`. Geometry is excluded by the simulator, so the relation between range and actual aura radius remains unassessed. Armament's first reach increase has low measured gain, while its capstone has demonstrated distant reach. Zero strict dominance is reported among 45 comparable crosspaths. There is no resource gap. Validity is supported for sampled checks; feasibility is partial because the output is bounded and accepted but provides no capability/omission explanation. Traceability is partial: source links for speed, impact and local stun are recoverable, while penetrating force and omitted families lack a decision rationale.

## S45

Source fidelity is partial. `/unit/actions/0` is a first-target projectile at range 32 equal to base, speed 100, damage 14, cooldown 0.9 and zero windup. All 28 distant probes do zero damage. The source supports a stretched slingshot punch, but there is no direct/line melee attack. Haki control is substantive: node 5 adds 0.45-second stun to Pistol and node 7 enables a four-target aura with 1.1-second stun every 4.5 seconds. The aura range is 26 and radius 12; true area geometry remains unassessed. Observation is missing, Armament is represented through damage/coating and a costed compressed strike, and Gear Second/Third-like changes are distributed across numerical upgrades. Complete distinct Gear forms are absent. In a hidden track, these are adaptation losses rather than violations of an explicit design brief.

Gameplay differentiation is supported with limits. Rubber Rush changes cadence, damage and emissions; Haki Command adds automatic control/collateral effects; Gear Ascension adds a manual resource-costed burst and later an external awakening attack. `/unit/resources/0` starts with two stamina, replenishes on Pistol hits and refills on wave. Node 10 unlocks stamina; node 11 grants its ability. Tier-2 crosspaths can reach the consumer, so there is no permanently unreachable crosspath resource consumer like R73's tier-3-only consumer. Buying tier 1 alone still has no benefit in sampled metrics.

Prose/mechanic correspondence is partial. Gear Burst has strongest targeting, projectile range 28, a real 0.25 windup, 48 damage later increased to 66, and a one-stamina action cost. The ability gates it to 12-second recovery despite the action's 0.6 cooldown. `/evidence/formProbes/0/variants/0/simulations/4` records exactly one stamina spent and 66 Gear damage in burst, matching the one-stamina promise. The primary's animation asks for wind-back, but its mechanical windup is zero, so any delay there is visual only. The explicit Knockback Authority name and positive displacement 5 at node 8 need direction evidence, which is absent. Other positive displacement values similarly cannot establish motion away from exit.

The external Fifth form at `/unit/forms/0` grants action 3, an aura with six target capacity, 20 damage, 4.5 displacement, cooldown 2.4 and range 34/radius 14. Form probes show durable damage 476 without versus 736 with the configuration, burst 192 versus 272. This is a real extra attack tied loosely to awakened environmental freedom, not a full rubberized-environment or runtime transformation system. The prose honestly says bounded configuration and disclaims state transfer. Source organization is partial because one form is separate but all Haki families are not three paths. Traceability is partial, with explicit unsupported-weakness and form limitations but no complete decision map.

Upgrade usefulness is partial. Findings 0, 5 and 6 show no-gain resource-only purchases; finding 3 shows no gain from Gear burst displacement. A Rubber-5 crosspath's added Haki pierce regresses aggregate utility while adding 25.65 fast damage and losing 3.6 grouped status target-seconds with seven more targeting failures, at finding 8. One of 45 comparable build pairs is dominated: Rubber 5 plus Gear 1 loses to Rubber 5 plus Haki 1 on measured status uptime. This is local evidence, not universal balance. Validity passes separately across all sampled checks, and feasibility is supported for the declared bounded mechanics. Missing source coverage remains a different issue.

## S18

This is a mechanically accepted candidate that leaves an explicit part of the brief unmet. `/unit/forms` is empty. Gear Second is a cadence upgrade, Third and Fourth are Emission damage upgrades, and Fifth is a separately enabled attack. There are no encounter Gear forms, no Gear Fourth variants and no form-unlocked attacks. Neither `/unit/summary` nor the empty requirements section explains why those requested capabilities were omitted. The default's lack of runtime transformations does not justify silently dropping supported external forms. Therefore source organization is supported for the three Haki paths but contradicted for the requested form organization. The request validator's pass is not proof that this natural-language requirement was fulfilled.

Source fidelity is partial overall, with supported distant melee and substantive Haki adaptations. Base `/unit/actions/0` is first-target direct-strike at range 30, damage 28, cooldown 1.2 and windup 0.15. Observation node 0 enables action 1, which combines a direct punch with two-second reveal, range 36 and cooldown 1. The path extends this to 48 then 60 and shortens cooldown to 0.5. `/evidence/distantTargetProbe/5/report/damageByAction/a-1` records 480 damage at distance 50. This is executable extended melee and presence detection. Reveal plus rapid cadence does not literally predict future moves, but has more source substance than a renamed ordinary strike.

Armament node 5 enables range-28 line-strike Emission with 48 damage, 1.35 cooldown, 0.25 windup and initial delivery cap two. Third/Fourth-inspired upgrades increase damage to 72 then 96, and capacity reaches five then eight. That supports projected force, heavy hits and multi-enemy penetration. It does not prove canonical Internal Destruction's bypass of defenses or internal anatomy. Conqueror node 10 grants a manual 12-second aura with 0.5 windup and two-second stun, range 26/radius 12. Its text says weak-willed, but no target filters implement that restriction. Node 11 enables a strongest-target coated direct strike at range 38, 44 damage every 1.2 seconds. Node 12 enables a 68-damage range-46 direct strike inspired by Fifth; that is an additive power move, not awakening's environmental freedom. Later nodes set two and three emissions, giving barrage claims actual repeated strikes.

Gameplay differentiation is supported with limits. The branches provide distant sustained reveal, line damage/collateral and a manual control tool plus heavy repeated blows. Upgrade usefulness is partial: Armament's increase to five hit capacity slightly regresses measured utility through four extra grouped targeting failures at finding 1; its final increase to eight yields zero measured gain at finding 3. Observation range increases have low aggregate gains but the final reach works in the dedicated distant probe. No resource gap exists, and zero of 45 comparable crosspaths show strict dominance. Geometry, weak-willed eligibility and defense bypass remain unassessed or unsupported as noted above.

Prose correspondence is partial: direct delivery, windups, range and repeated emissions match claims, while future prediction and weak-willed filtering do not. Feasibility is partial despite passing the profile: the unit fits the declared default, but it does not honestly explain the missing requested form capabilities. Traceability is partial: the broad speed/enlargement/compression/coating inspirations can be followed from research to operations, while their replacement of requested forms is unexplained. Validity is supported for sampled checks. Those separate facts should survive any comparison.

## Compact comparisons

Within hidden replicate 1, S24 versus S45 has no overall winner. S24 has stronger executable melee reach: direct strikes reach 52 and hit the 50-unit probe, whereas S45's projectile primary never reaches it. S45 has stronger form organization and broader Gear adaptation: an external Fifth form demonstrably unlocks an extra attack, with a costed compressed burst and explicit capability limits. S24 instead omits forms and Observation, and three late Conqueror purchases have zero sampled gains. S45 introduces a no-gain resource prerequisite and one measured dominated crosspath, but its tier-2 consumer is reachable. Both pass validation; gameplay preference depends on sustained speed/damage/manual stun versus automatic control/costed burst/awakened area attack. S82 supplies no unit and cannot be assigned a content loss to either.

Within brief replicate 1, S18 supplies valid executed melee and Haki jobs but does not satisfy the requested forms. S67 supplies richer declared form organization and more explicit capability limits but fails schema before any executable evaluation. These dimensions are incomparable as an overall result: S18 is usable under sampled checks with a missing requirement; S67 is a rejected attempt at that requirement. S39 supplies no unit, so content comparisons with it are insufficient evidence.

Across brief replicates, S18 and R58 both demonstrate distant direct melee. R58 additionally gives each requested Gear configuration a real form-enabled attack and provides per-form simulation gains, but fails the unchanged mechanic limit. S18 fits that limit by delivering a smaller design, without explaining the missing form requirements. This is evidence of a feasibility/adaptation tradeoff in these samples, not evidence that either fulfills the complete brief. R84 has richer source-specific detection and weak-willed eligibility than S18, but uses projectiles and also fails complexity. R29 offers a form-replacement design whose disable operations conflict; unlike R58/R84, it has no simulation evidence. No preferred-brief candidate in the reviewed R/S set simultaneously demonstrates accepted validity, distant melee, all requested form distinctions and honest complete handling of omissions.

Across hidden replicates, S45 avoids R73's permanently stranded Gear crosspath resource because its consumer is granted at tier 2 rather than 3. Both have executable Fifth attack forms and projectile primary loops. S24's real direct melee is a source adaptation absent from the earlier four hidden R units, but its sparse Gear/Haki coverage should not disappear when that advantage is reported. Replicate labels are not matched seeds; these are concrete artifact comparisons, not statistical rankings.

Cost and latency are unassessed for every new packet. Missing accounting is unknown. Execution success/failure and missing-unit outcomes are reported in the table without assigning invented quality scores.

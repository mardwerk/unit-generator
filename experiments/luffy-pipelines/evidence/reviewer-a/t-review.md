# T packet review

All five reviewed T candidates pass the supplied schema/semantic, profile, request and sampled compilation checks. None implements forms or resources. Their decision supplements explicitly identify a narrower prototype capability set that excludes encounter forms and runtime states; those exclusions are not limitations of the full default DSL. Omission disclosure is real and should receive credit. It does not fulfill a form requirement or establish that every omitted attack abstraction was impossible.

I read unit mechanics before the decision supplements. All supplied mapping pointers resolve, but many mappings cite unrelated knowledge claims. The source record remains the same 43 claims, so pointer resolution and common source IDs must not be treated as traceability success. No overall quality score or accounting comparison is made.

## T37

Source fidelity is partial. `/unit/actions/0` is first-target direct Pistol, range 24, damage 32, cooldown 1.4, windup 0.15. Gear Second path lowers cooldown to 0.48 and remains above the 0.15 trigger floor, supporting real speed progression. Third action 1 is a strongest-target direct blow, initial range 28, damage 110, cooldown 3.2 and windup 0.45; tier 5 raises it to 320 damage/range 44. The larger slower prepared hit meaningfully adapts bone inflation, rather than just attaching a Gear name to equal behavior. Emission action 2 is a manual direct blast, initially range 20, damage 150 and 0.35 windup, gated by an 18-second ability; later reaches 36 and 420 damage. This is a declared projected-Haki abstraction, not defense bypass. All 28 distant probes are zero, consistent with maximum automatic reach 44 and abilities disabled. Shorter direct melee still expresses elastic range; 50 is not a fidelity threshold.

Source organization is partial and alternate rather than disobedient: Gear Second, Third and Emission paths replace the withheld Haki organization. Decision omissions explicitly prioritize attacks over adding representable Conqueror stun. Observation predictive states are unavailable, but a reveal abstraction would still have been possible. Fourth/Fifth and forms are omitted through the declared subset. Gameplay differentiation is partial: rapid first-target sustained damage, slower strongest-target heavy damage and player-timed burst are distinct jobs, but no crowd control/detection survives. Numerical changes serve those jobs.

Upgrade usefulness is partial. Manual Emission range tier 3 has zero gain at `/evidence/diagnostics/reviewFindings/2`; several manual damage purchases have low aggregate gains but should be judged on burst-enabled scenarios. The first speed crosspath on Third-5 slightly regresses utility, at finding 6. Two of 45 comparable build pairs are dominated. No resource gaps exist. Prose correspondence is partial: real windups and cadence support their claims, while Internal Destruction at node 13 merely sets damage to 300 and does not implement penetration. Omissions make feasibility an honest bounded adaptation; validity passes separately.

Traceability is contradicted for several material mappings even though broad mechanics are source-plausible. In `review-decisions/T37.json`, `/sourceMappings/2` maps Emission to claims 31 and 32, which actually concern Conqueror incapacitation and Future Sight. Mapping 16 assigns Internal Destruction's damage upgrade to Future Sight claim 32. Correct relevant claims would include Emission 33 and Internal Destruction 34. The mapping for base Pistol also includes resistance claim 12, which does not entail an offensive strike. Citations need semantic checking.

## T26

Source fidelity is partial. Pistol action 0 is direct range 24, damage 34, cooldown 1.2 and windup 0.15. Gear Second reduces cooldown to 0.4. Armament changes damage and collateral capacity together, reaching 104 damage and six applications. Third action 1 is a strongest-target manual direct strike, range 28, 150 damage, 12-second recovery and 0.8 windup. Tier 4 adds a second emission; capstone raises damage to 260 and range to 40. A delayed double heavy strike is materially different from rapid regular punching. All distant probes are zero because automatic Pistol never extends beyond 24 and the manual strike is disabled in that probe.

Source organization is partial, with reasonable hidden-track speed/penetration/manual-burst jobs rather than three Haki branches. Conqueror coating is a damage/collateral upgrade inside Armament, not a separate control family; Observation and Fourth/Fifth forms are absent. Returned notes openly choose a minimal offense-focused design and omit knockout rather than claiming stun is impossible. Gear form and defensive limitations are explained as subset limits. This is an honest loss of breadth.

Gameplay differentiation and prose correspondence are supported for cadence, collateral capacity, manual windup and two direct emissions, but only partial for canon mechanics. Emission and Internal Impact are larger direct damage/collateral numbers, not a distinct projected blast or defense bypass. Upgrade usefulness is partial: Third tier 3 range expansion shows zero measured gain, and damage increases have small aggregate gains but real burst relevance. No regressing edges or dominance findings are reported among 45 comparable pairs. Absence of diagnostic regressions is not proof of balance. No resources exist.

Traceability is contradicted in the Haki portion. Mapping 7 cites fruit-renaming claim 27 and general strength claim 15 for Hardened Knuckles; mapping 8 cites Observation 30 and fruit-renaming 27 for Emission; mapping 10 cites Future Sight 32 and fruit-renaming 27 for Conqueror-coated damage. These are not equivalent source facts. Gear Second and Third mappings are closer, though drawback facts do not themselves entail damage improvements. The unit's plausible attacks cannot repair incorrect mapping provenance. Validity passes and feasibility is honest within the declared subset.

Source organization is partial: reach, speed/damage and Emission are clear offensive jobs, but Conqueror, Observation, Third/Fourth and Fifth are absent as distinct capabilities. The returned notes acknowledge that reveal is possible but not selected, and that exact weak-willed filters are absent. This is a reasoned hidden-track simplification, not a violation of a received Haki brief. Forms/resources cannot be produced by the declared subset. Generic fixed damage upgrades do not meaningfully distinguish Third's enlargement or Fourth's compression merely because the omission note calls them indirect representations.

Gameplay differentiation is partial because all paths remain offense-focused, with geometry/target collateral beyond the simulator unassessed. Upgrade usefulness is partial: the second reach purchase has zero gain at finding 0 and as a Haki-primary crosspath at finding 8; Emission pierce slightly regresses utility at finding 3, and unlocking short-range Emission on two other primary paths regresses aggregate utility at findings 5/6. Two of 44 comparable pairs are dominated. These observations do not make range globally useless. Prose largely matches explicit numerical operations and windup; the Internal Destruction capstone is damage only. No resources or forms exist, so no unreachable consumer is introduced. ## T81

The candidate receives the explicit brief. Its three Haki branches are substantively organized, but `/unit/forms` is empty and no characteristic Gear form actions exist. Returned notes explain the subset's lack of forms and propose concrete attack improvements instead. This is disclosed unmet scope, not silent omission and not complete fulfillment. The source's missing Fourth catalogue does not erase the known Second/Third/Fifth facts.

Source fidelity is partial with supported distant melee. Pistol action 0 is first-target direct range 35, damage 32, cooldown 1.2 and windup 0.15. Observation extends Pistol to 45 then 58. Distant probe 9 records 288 direct Pistol damage alongside 440 homing barrage damage at distance 50. The physical melee reach is thus real, independent of the projectile contribution. Future-Sight Barrage action 2 uses strongest-target homing, two emissions increasing to three, range 42 then 58, cooldown 1.1 then 0.8 and windup 0.2. This is guided repeated offense, not literal prediction or presence detection.

Conqueror action 1 is manual area/aura with 24 damage and 1.5-second stun, 18-second recovery, windup 0.4, range 24 and radius 10. It later gains eight emissions, 75 damage and range 36. Armament action 3 is line-strike, range 30, 48 damage, cap three, cooldown 2.4 and windup 0.3; upgrades take cap six, 115 damage and range 42, while a separate upgrade raises base Pistol damage to 48. These control/guided/sweeping-damage jobs are real and distinct. No weakness filters or defense-bypass mechanics are present.

Prose correspondence is contradicted for one exact promise. `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/4/summary` says the capstone increases damage and stun duration. Its operations modify only damage to 75 and action range to 36; `/unit/actions/1/effects/1/durationSeconds` remains 1.5. This is a concrete claim/operation mismatch, not an unmodeled canon interpretation. 'Expands affected area' at node 2 only increases action range while aura radius stays 10, so geometry is unassessed. Increasing emissions at node 3 supports repeated applications but does not independently prove more distinct enemies; the summary's target-count promise needs a suitable probe.

Upgrade usefulness is partial: Conqueror tier 3 range has zero gain, Observation early range and Armament pierce have slight regressions, and several crosspath changes have low aggregate utility. Zero of 45 comparable pairs are dominated. Forms/resources are absent, so no consumer/form reachability is claimed. Validity passes; feasibility is honest within the subset but leaves the form brief unmet.

Traceability is contradicted for several Haki links. Mapping 1 assigns Conqueror Burst to fruit-renaming claim 27 and generic three-Haki claim 28, omitting the actual incapacitation claim 31. Mapping 2 assigns Future-Sight Barrage to Armament claim 29 plus elastic claims. Mapping 17 links Internal Destruction pierce to Conqueror unconsciousness claim 31. These are wrong source links, not just conservative adaptations.

## T54

The received brief is only partly met. Three Haki branches exist with manual area control, guided/reveal targeting and projected damage, but Gear forms and family distinctions are absent. Returned omissions candidly explain the restricted subset and incomplete named attack catalogue. Permanent action unlocks remain available in that subset, so absence of every Gear-themed attack is a chosen loss of content, not proof the full default cannot represent them.

Source fidelity and gameplay differentiation are partial. Pistol action 0 is first-target direct at range 28, damage 24, cooldown 1.2 and windup 0.15, with no reach upgrades. The ranged Observation action 2 is a first-target homing projectile, range 34 then 42, damage 20 then 32, cooldown 1.5, windup 0.1 and three-second reveal. Later it has two emissions and two-target delivery. Emission action 3 is a strongest-target projectile, range 30 then 38, damage 42 then 90, cooldown 2.2 and windup 0.25, later two-target delivery. Every beyond-base reach expansion occurs on projectile attacks; there is no improved direct/line melee reach. All 28 50-unit probes do zero damage. That probe alone is not a quality threshold, but the direct-range versus projectile-range distinction matters for this brief.

Conqueror action 1 has manual 16-second recovery, 0.5 windup, aura radius/range 16, 30 damage and 2.5-second stun. Upgrades raise damage to 70, action range to 20 and emissions to two. The text sticks to damage, range and emissions, avoiding T81's nonexistent stun-duration improvement. Future Sight has actual reveal and homing, and Internal Destruction is explicitly a damage adaptation rather than anatomy/defense bypass. Source organization is supported for branch jobs and contradicted for requested forms, with honest external disclosure.

Upgrade usefulness is partial: Emission tier 3 range has zero gain in its primary path and one crosspath, at findings 2 and 9. Most remaining reported low gains concern damage/manual burst or range, not proof of dead mechanics. No regressions or dominated pairs among 45 comparable builds are reported. No resources exist. Prose geometry and literal future prediction remain unassessed; accepted schema/profile does not certify them.

Traceability is contradicted in its Haki links. Mapping 1 connects Conqueror's aura to Observation/Future Sight claims 30/32. Mapping 2 cites Emission/Internal Destruction 33/34 for Future-Sight Pistol. Mapping 18 cites poison immunity claim 36 for Internal Destruction damage. The actual research record contains appropriate Haki claims, so these errors are avoidable mapping failures rather than missing source coverage.

## Comparative recommendation from these samples

For the brief track, retain S18 as the strongest accepted example of the requested combat structure among the S/T candidates reviewed so far. Its direct Observation strike reaches 60, its reveal is substantive, and its line Armament plus Conqueror coating/barrage have concrete jobs. Its returned mapping records largely reference the right source claims. It still cannot be called a full brief success because it omits encounter forms under the chosen restricted subset.

T81 is a useful alternative for mixed direct/homing reach and manual burst, but its capstone's false stun-duration promise and wrong Haki mapping indices are concrete regressions in correspondence/traceability relative to S18. T54 has reveal and honest prose, but its expanded reach belongs to projectiles while direct melee stays at 28, and it has similarly wrong mapping indices. Neither supplies Gear forms. This recommendation rests on executed delivery and source-link accuracy, not how many named mechanics appear.

For the hidden track, T37 and T26 offer understandable speed/heavy/manual-damage adaptations; T26's longer prepared double Third punch is particularly clear. Their sparse control/Observation/form coverage is an explicitly chosen alternative organization. They do not displace S24 for demonstrated extended direct melee or S45/R73 for executed form-unlocked awakening attacks. T92's reach growth is straightforward and can be useful despite zero at the 50-unit probe, but its family coverage and source mappings are weaker. There is no evidence here of a consistent content improvement across candidates just because more planning/citation fields were returned.

The primary recurring issue in these five packets is incorrect claim indexing, not missing citation syntax. Repairing that provenance would improve reviewability but would not add the omitted capabilities. Costs, call reliability beyond reported outcomes, and latency remain unassessed.

## T63, added after the first five reviews

T63 is a successful brief-track replicate-1 candidate with all supplied validity checks passed. It is the strongest T example of retaining distinct Gear attack behavior, and its mappings mostly identify the correct source families. It still has no forms. Its explicit returned omissions describe permanent path-enabled attacks rather than Gear switching, and its declared subset cannot produce encounter forms. This is an explained lossy implementation of the brief, not a full form implementation.

Source fidelity is partial, with concrete family distinctions. Pistol action 0 is a first-target direct strike, base range 32, damage 34, cooldown 1.2 and windup 0.15. Observation extends its range to 40 and speeds it to 0.95. Second action 3 is another direct punch with 0.7 cooldown and 0.1 windup, range 34 and damage 26. Third action 5 is a strongest-target direct blow, 72 damage later 96, range 36, 2.4 cooldown and 0.35 windup. Fourth action 6 is a strongest-target line strike, initial damage 92, range 48, 3.2 cooldown, 0.45 windup and collateral cap two, later damage 130/cap five. Those differences implement speed versus prepared enlarged impact versus projected compressed force. This is meaningful adaptation, not simply more Gear names.

Fifth action 2 uses zone delivery with radius 8 and lifetime 0.35, area targeting, range 38, 110 damage every 5 seconds and 0.55 windup. Conqueror tier 5 enables it as an independent action. It has a distinct declared area-impact behavior, though the simulator does not establish world geometry or environmental rubberization. Conqueror also grants a manual 28-second aura with 2.5-second stun and 0.6 windup, and increases base punch damage/collateral capacity as coating. The aura summary claims up to eight enemies while delivery capacity is one; a dedicated multi-enemy action trace is needed to verify that promise. Future Sight action 4 uses strongest-target homing plus two-second reveal, 30 damage, range 46 and cooldown 0.9 later 0.65. This gives actual detection and guided targeting without proving future prediction.

Source organization is supported for three Haki branches and partial for keeping known Gear adaptations, but contradicted for the explicit external-form requirement. No Fourth variants are present; the notes correctly cite incomplete variant evidence. All 28 distance-50 probes are zero, since the longest line strike reaches 48 and no automatic action reaches 50. This does not invalidate elastic melee at 40 to 48, but it gives less distant-target evidence than S18's range-60 direct strike or T81's range-58 Pistol. Gameplay differentiation is supported for manual control, fast/homing detection and heavy/line offense. No resources or runtime state lifecycle exists.

Upgrade usefulness is partial. Armament's cap increase to five mildly regresses utility at `/evidence/diagnostics/reviewFindings/3`; adding Observation tier 2 range regresses on Conqueror-5 and Armament-5 crosspaths at findings 4 and 9. Low gains include base cadence and heavy line damage improvements. These are fixed-scenario observations, not globally dead purchases. Zero of the reported comparable pairs are dominated. Exact ranges and timings in summaries correspond to operations, while literal internal defense destruction remains a bounded damage abstraction. Feasibility is honest for the chosen subset; validity is independently supported.

Traceability is stronger than the first five T packets. Mapping 4 links homing/reveal to Observation/Future Sight claims 30/32; mapping 6 links Fourth compression to muscle inflation/compression/Armament/Emission/Internal Destruction claims 20/21/29/33/34; mapping 13 links reach to Observation/Future Sight. These use the actual shared claim indices. Some links remain weak abstractions: Conqueror coating does not directly entail collateral pierce, and awakened freedom does not specifically entail a fixed zone dealing 110 damage. A correct index identifies an inspiration, not proof that the source requires the resulting game operation.

For brief-track review, retain both S18 and T63 as useful accepted partial adaptations. Prefer S18 when the main review question is demonstrated distant direct melee and a compact Haki combat loop. Prefer T63 when the question is preserving distinct Second/Third/Fourth/Fifth attack characteristics within the restricted subset. Neither meets the requested form-unlocked attack structure. T63 improves on the other T samples' source mapping accuracy, so the earlier five-packet mapping failure must not be generalized to all T outcomes.

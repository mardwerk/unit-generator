# Additional blinded review, provisional

This supplements `provisional-review.md` with R73, R58, R84 and R11. R73 is hidden track replicate 2. R58, R84 and R11 are brief track replicate 2. Failed candidates are retained as evidence of attempted design, not accepted units. Pairwise comparisons remain within track/replicate.

## Source supplement and correction

I inspected the captured Wikipedia source in `source-material.json`, `/sources/0/content`, source ID `505dd462a59de785c62a62ca`, URL `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey_D._Luffy`. This is a secondary-source entailment check, not an audit against primary manga chapters. It supersedes the earlier statement that source text was unavailable. The source confirms stretched/slingshot punches at distance, Gear Second speed and strain, Third bone inflation, Fourth muscle inflation plus Armament compression, Fifth environmental rubberization and white appearance, Observation presence/movement reading and Future Sight, Armament attack/defense enhancement plus Emission/Internal Destruction, and Conqueror incapacitation of weak-willed nearby targets plus later coating. Those mechanical connections in the earlier review stand.

The captured source does not mention Bounceman, Boundman, Tankman, Snakeman or Kong Gun. A candidate may draw on wider canon, but those names and variant-specific attributes are not established by this shared evidence. The shared research preserves the source's known limits instead of turning a partial catalogue into a requirement. Exact numeric ranges and timings are game design choices. Source facts do not require exactly three Haki branches; only the brief track does.

A later packet also corrects one early targeting inference. R73's Gatling delivery cap increases from one to two while its targeting cap stays one, yet the supplied simulator records additional hits and a kill in fast. Thus unchanged targeting cap alone does not prove R62's Gear Smash delivery-cap increase cannot affect extra enemies. The earlier review has been amended. R62's increase has zero measured gain in the supplied scenarios; its wider capability remains unassessed without a suitable target-count probe. This reviewer does not infer engine semantics from field names when executable evidence conflicts.

## R73, hidden replicate 2

**Source fidelity: partial.** Primary `/unit/actions/0` uses first targeting, projectile speed 42, range 28 equal to base, 18 kinetic damage, cooldown 1.05 and windup 0.08. Rubber tier 1 extends its reach to 32. Gatling `/unit/actions/1` has three projectiles per cycle, 0.55 cooldown and range 30, so the barrage is substantive. Observation at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/5` adds on-hit reveal, tied to presence detection. Haki tier 3 adds a 0.9-second stun and explicitly calls it a bounded generalized pressure stagger rather than unrestricted Conqueror incapacitation. This acknowledges the missing weak-willed susceptibility rule, but the three Haki families remain partly conflated. Armament Emission `/unit/actions/2` is an independent strongest-target projectile, range 32, 28 Haki damage and 2.8-second cooldown, enabled at node 8. Its separate projected blast has a stronger connection to `/knowledge/claims/33` than a mere damage-name change. Piercing Emission at node 9 multiplies damage by 1.7 and explicitly calls it a bounded damage increase; this does not implement internal defense penetration.

Gear Second circulation is initially resource generation at node 10, followed by cadence at node 11. Source Gear Second's energy strain does not specifically support gaining stamina by hitting enemies, so that is a game economy invention. Gear Fourth's manually invoked compressed punch at `/unit/actions/3` has 54 damage, 14-second recovery, 0.35 windup and a four-stamina cost. It has a source connection to compression, while the exact Kong Gun/Boundman attribution is not established by the provided source. Third is omitted. **Source organization: partial.** `/unit/upgradeGraph/paths` groups rubber reach, general Haki and Gear escalation. This is a reasonable hidden-track design with distinct jobs, but the Haki powers are not three branches. The one external form is structurally distinct from the upgrades that grant it. The research itself retains the more complete power-family organization.

**Gameplay differentiation: supported with limits.** Rubber gives repeated hits and later an independent barrage, Haki gives reveal/stun plus strongest-target emission, and Gear gives a stamina-costed manual burst plus an encounter-selected zone attack. Resource spending is real: `/evidence/formProbes/0/variants/0/simulations/4` records 4 stamina spent and 86.4 ability damage in burst. `/evidence/diagnostics/reviewFindings/4` records a further 32.4 burst and 64.8 support damage from compressed force. **Upgrade usefulness: partial.** Gear tier 1 enables stamina and on-hit generation, yet its only consumer arrives at tier 3. Because crosspath depth is capped at two, any non-Gear primary path that chooses Gear tier 1 or 2 can never spend that stamina in a legal build. This is a genuine reachable resource-to-consumer gap at `/unit/resources/0`, `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/10`, `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/12` and `/unit/upgradeGraph/selectionRules`. It is not merely failure of a short scenario to use an unlocked resource. Findings 0, 8 and 9 show zero utility for these resource-only purchases. Gear tier 2 does improve primary cadence but leaves the crosspath resource unused.

Reveal tiers 1 and 2 and several reveal crosspath purchases have zero measured utility; the reach purchase also has zero gain in base and some crosspaths. These are six-scenario limitations. Gatling's target-cap purchase adds 24 fast damage and one kill despite low aggregate utility at finding 5, so it should not be called dead. `/evidence/diagnostics/evidence/8` reports 4 dominated pairs of 45 comparisons. Findings 15 through 18 identify them exactly, including Rubber tier 2 over Gear tier 2 and Haki tier 2 at equal 1120 cost, Rubber tier 3 over Gear tier 3 at 1770, and awakened Gear 5 plus Rubber 1 over awakened Gear 5 plus Haki 1 at 5570. The last dominance rests on one fewer fast targeting failure, not a dramatic combat improvement. All findings and scenarios are preserved in the companion JSON.

**Prose/mechanic correspondence: partial.** Gatling's three projectile cycle and 0.55-second cooldown support barrage wording. The manual Fourth strike has an actual windup and reachable stamina cost. The Fifth form enables `/unit/actions/4`, a first-target zone with three selected/delivered targets, radius 8, lifetime 0.8, cooldown 0.9, range 28 and 24 damage plus positive displacement 3. Form probes show durable damage 612 without versus 1428 with the form, burst 248.4 versus 464.4. This is a meaningful new attack unlocked by form selection. It is not merely a renamed base attack.

The zone summary says it knocks enemies back, while its displacement is positive 3. The packet does not contain a position trace or signed-direction contract, so defensive motion away from exit is unassessed. Its radius/lifetime are declared but the protocol excludes geometry, so zone-shaped combat cannot be certified from damage alone. The same caution applies to Rubber Recoil's 'away' wording and positive displacement 2. Both promises deserve a movement-direction probe before approval.

**Feasibility: partial, with honest capability limits.** It passes the supplied three-mechanic profile despite locked ability/form and control categories. `/unit/summary` admits unmodeled contextual weaknesses and resistances, while `/unit/forms/0` accurately uses external activation and encounter persistence. No runtime switch is claimed. The source-to-game resource interpretation and Third omission are losses, but not default contract violations. No competing path writes to the same owned property are evidenced.

**Validity: supported for sampled checks.** All issue arrays at `/evidence/validity` are empty. The form-selected build and manually costed ability simulate. This does not verify gameplay direction, collisions or the runtime integration.

**Traceability: partial.** Reveal, projected Emission, compressed burst and a rubber-zone awakening have recoverable source links, now checked against the captured text. The stamina-on-hit economy and exact Kong Gun/Boundman name need a source or explicit adaptation rationale. No complete claim-to-decision audit is supplied.

**Cost, latency and reliability: unassessed.** This attempt has a successful unit. Accounting is withheld.

This turns detection into a targeting dependency rather than merely naming an ordinary strongest-target strike Future Sight. It still does not predict future positions or grant dodge. The five forms enable Second rapid attacks, Third heavy hits, Fourth power and homing pursuit variants, and Fifth area impact/displacement. This is a concrete ownership decision, not generic organization prose. The two Fourth configurations acknowledge incomplete canonical coverage rather than fulfilling the entire requested variant set.

**Gameplay differentiation: partial.** The control, detection/marked-target and damage branches have distinct operations. Conqueror's job cannot be observed on the supplied untagged scenarios, while forms and other actions contribute damage. Form probes 0 through 4, variants 0/1 simulation 0, show durable damage with/without Fifth 1434/924, Second 2698/1258, Fourth pursuit 2474/1258, Third 2106/1238 and Fourth power 2428/1238. Forms are additive external attack configurations. They do not replace base attacks or implement runtime switching.

**Upgrade usefulness: partial.** The companion JSON preserves all 13 low/zero/regressing findings and 0/45 measured crosspath dominance. Conqueror stun-duration purchases show zero benefit because no eligible weak-willed targets are demonstrated, and aura unlocks add failures. These tests cannot adjudicate its intended control job. The scan can mark before the marked-strike consumer arrives at the next tier, but that is a status-targeting dependency, not a resource gap. No resources exist. Extra attacks and form grants show damage gains, but the candidate remains rejected for complexity.

**Prose/mechanic correspondence: partial.** Source-specific eligibility, reveal/marks, marked targeting, real windups, distinct rapid/heavy timings and form unlocks are supported by operations and probes. Stronger defense penetration is unassessed. Fourth pursuit is a homing projectile and its description admits it as an adaptation. Fifth `/unit/actions/9` uses area/aura, radius 14 but action range 55, damage 34 and displacement -6. The distant probe reports Fifth damage at 50 even though aura geometry is excluded by the evaluator. This cannot establish a radius-14 aura affecting a distant target in a real engine. Signed motion away from exit also requires a movement trace or contract semantics; a negative value alone is not independently verified here.

**Traceability: partial, stronger explicit abstraction handling.** Most family mechanics match the captured source. The forms explain unmodeled strain, shrinking, runtime transitions and awakening. Power/pursuit Fourth configurations acknowledge the variant evidence gap, and internal damage is bounded rather than claiming full canon defense bypass. Exact durations, tags and branch prerequisites remain design choices rather than source entailments. No complete claim-to-operation map is supplied.

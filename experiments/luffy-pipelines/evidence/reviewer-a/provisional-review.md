Observation has substantive detection and control at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/6`, `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/7` and `/unit/actions/1`. The independent action targets the strongest enemy at range 42 every 3.2 seconds and applies reveal for 3 seconds and a 25% slow for 2 seconds. This connects weakly but concretely to Armament's attack enhancement in `/knowledge/claims/29`; it lacks defensive coating, defense penetration or internal destruction. Conqueror's incapacitation or coating from `/knowledge/claims/31` and `/knowledge/claims/35` is absent.

Gear Second is a cadence upgrade at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/10`, grounded in `/knowledge/claims/16`. Gear Third unlocks one enlarged direct strike through `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/12` and `/unit/abilities/0`, grounded in `/knowledge/claims/18`. **Source organization: partial.** `/unit/upgradeGraph/paths` groups force, Observation control and Gear tempo. The jobs are readable, but this does not organize around three Haki specializations. `/unit/forms/0` keeps the one form structurally separate from upgrade nodes, although the Gear family spans upgrades, an ability and a form. The shared research preserves stretch, Haki families, Gears and limitations as facts without declaring that any must be separate game branches.

**Gameplay differentiation: supported with limits.** Elastic force adds damage, displacement and emissions. Observation adds detection, slowing and a strongest-target ranged control action. Elastic tier 2 displacement regresses utility, losing one grouped kill while adding 4 damage, and the same issue appears in two crosspaths. Observation tier 1 has low aggregate gain but adds one hit and 14 damage in fast. The Gear Fourth-tier damage upgrade is low in the aggregate yet adds the burst/support damage noted above. These are scenario-local findings, not declarations that reveal or burst upgrades are globally useless. `/unit/resources` is empty; no resource unlock-to-consumer gap exists.

**Prose/mechanic correspondence: partial.** Rapid limbs really sets two projectiles per cycle at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/3/operations/0`; Wide rubber barrage then raises the delivery target cap to two, with a targeting cap of two already present. Treat that as visual staging only, not an implemented charge delay. Elastic displacement uses positive 3 at `/unit/upgradeGraph/nodes/1/operations/0/effect/distanceWorldUnits`. The text promises displacement, not explicitly knockback away from the exit; direction is not established by the packet and must not be credited as defensive knockback.

The Gear Third action is reachable through the granted ability despite `unlockedByDefault:false` on the action. `/evidence/formProbes/0/variants/1/simulations/4/damageByAction/gear-third-strike` records 93.5 burst damage. The prose honestly says faster attacks rather than claiming implemented environmental transformation.

Wider firing angles at node 3 are unassessed geometry: emitter count alone establishes neither spread angles nor extra aggregate emissions. Unlike R47, it does not explain which source capabilities were dropped because of the default contract. This is an underspecified, lossy adaptation rather than a false claim that all Gear forms work. Separate branches alter different action properties, so no ownership conflict is evidenced.

**Validity: supported for sampled checks.** All supplied validity issue arrays are empty. The zero-gain target-cap purchase is a scenario usefulness concern within accepted output; a structural impossibility is not established.

**Traceability: partial.** Elastic punch, enlarged strike and local stunning pressure have reconstructible research links. Vulnerability-as-Observation, reveal-on-Conqueror and generic Gear naming blur source families. No explicit adaptation record or source-grounded omission rationale bridges the research to these choices.

**Cost, latency and reliability: unassessed.** No accounting is available in the blinded packet. This attempt has a successful unit.
